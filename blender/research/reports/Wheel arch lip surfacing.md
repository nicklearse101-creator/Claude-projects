# Build the arch as a ring, not a slice

Professional modellers do not surface a wheel arch as part of the side panel. They build an **over-built parent side surface**, trim the opening out of it, and then add a separate **ring-shaped arch band** whose sections all pivot about **one arch centre**. A **blend or fillet** (G2 where the highlight must flow, G1 where the geometry forbids more) connects the band to the side, and a **straight flange turned about 90 degrees inboard** finishes the lip. Alias, Rhino and polygon (Kunos-style) practice all agree on this, with tool names changing between packages. The arch outline itself is a side-view arc about the wheel centre, projected onto the body side. For reverse engineering from a mesh, the same idea applies: parameterise the lip band **polar-style, as (arc length round the opening) x (distance out from the real edge)**, fit it as a tensor B-spline in those coordinates, and pin the first two pole rows to the panel to get G1. Our own test on the Kunos AE86 front wing backs this up: the polar fit reached **1.64 mm p95 on the 0-70 mm band, against 13 mm** for the old along-the-car band. The sources give almost no production numbers (lip radii, flange depths, blend-width laws), so those values have to come from measuring the mesh.

## One centre drives the whole arch layout

The Autodesk Alias "Wheel Arch of a Car" tutorial starts by fitting a circle to the mesh arch in side view and using its centre as the **pivot for every section**. It uses "as few sections as possible, with one at the top (12 o'clock)", lines sections up with body shut lines to hide tangency breaks, and builds the inner arch as a railed surface from a three-point arc fitted to the mesh. It works side-panel continuity first and leaves curvature between the new surfaces for later. It deletes the inside surfaces at the end ([Autodesk Alias help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/GUID-545A5B42-8EC5-479D-9739-6C4ACE21DE2B.htm)). Rhino practitioners describe the same structure. Either trim the opening from the basic shape, or **arrange the transverse sections radially around it** and surface with Sweep2 or NetworkSrf, with the **inboard flange as a one-rail sweep** joined to the basic shape ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/approach-to-very-curvy-car-fenders/35114)). McNeel staff add the ordering rule: trim into a **clean, over-built main surface** and build the small surfaces to meet it, never the reverse ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/srf-match-challenge-unsolved/98637)).

LearningAlias shows the production variant. Build a "square" eyebrow surface where the flange goes. Cut both it and the body side with a tubular surface, trim back to leave a **deliberate gap**, and fill the gap with a Free Form Fillet, which on a rear arch running up into the shoulder was built in **three pieces** ([LearningAlias: Wheel Arch Blending](https://blog.learningalias.co.uk/2010/02/24/autodesk-alias-building-a-car-body/)). The difficult spot is the top of the arch, where the trim edge meets the parent patch. One practitioner keeps a **clean four-sided structure** with an "anti-tangent" vertex (about 10 degrees of tangent difference) or a trimmed five-sided CV surface, and rejects power fills, which force G2 at the boundary but leave an uneven interior ([LearningAlias: Anti Tangent](https://blog.learningalias.co.uk/?p=4073)). General Class-A rules apply too. Use few, long patches, consistent U/V directions, and 4-way convergences, and put star points in low-reflection zones ([Novedge](https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/alias-patch-layout-mastery-5-class-a-strategies-for-clean-highlights-stable-edits-and-engineering-ready-surfaces)).

Polygon modellers reach the same topology by a different route. Polycount advice is to "make the topology a full loop around wheel arches," with **concentric rings** parallel to the lip and poles and triangles kept off the arch edge ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2703491/); [Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/car-rim-topology-problem/619004)). Nik's kn5 study of shipped Kunos cars found exactly this: concentric loops round the arches, 2-3 tight parallel loops on each feature line, an even grid of about 20 mm on the AE86, and crispness from loop spacing rather than hard edges (3-7 % hard edges on Kunos cars against 20-37 % on mods) (local `D:\Claude\Claude-\blender\kn5-study\KN5_STUDY.md` §4). In Blender the outline comes from a circle at the wheel centre with the body bridged to it. Booleans are left out or applied late, and shrinkwrap to a smooth reference avoids pinching ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2020/06/15/daily-blender-tip-car-modeling-basics-part-3/)).

The working layout for code follows from all of this:

| Piece | What it is | Parameterisation |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | Parent side surface, untrimmed, over-built past the opening | Native panel (u,v) |
| S1 | Arch/lip ring band, 2-3 spans, split at 12 o'clock | u = arc length round the arch, v = across the lip, radial about the centre |
| F | Blend between S0's trim edge and S1 (optional if S1 is G1-pinned to S0) | Same u spans as S1 |
| R | Inboard return flange | One-rail sweep along the opening edge |

The 2-3 span count is an inference from the three-piece Alias fillet, not a sourced rule.

## Two lines define a lip: the highlight line and the cut edge

The Alias rolled-edge tools make the distinction that matters for reconstruction. **Tube Flange** places a tube touching a contact line "where highlights change", which is the start of the roll on the side surface ([Alias 2015](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2015/ENU/Alias-Modeling/files/Create_rolled_edge_surfaces.htm)). **Fillet Flange** treats its input boundary as "the finished component's specification", the edge that must be kept ([Alias 2024: Fillet Flange](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-6C6C9216-B6EB-4FAF-B8DA-D62CB5E5831B.html)). A lip is the band between these two curves-on-surface, and its width is their offset along S0. The only sourced width is LearningAlias's **25 mm flange**, projected onto the body side and then pushed out to "where the wheel's outer edge will sit", so the flare is placed against the tyre and not just the panel ([LearningAlias](https://blog.learningalias.co.uk/2010/02/24/autodesk-alias-building-a-car-body/)). No source builds the outline as an offset of the tyre envelope. Practice is a side-view arc about the wheel centre plus a clearance check.

There are three families of construction. **Rolled-edge tools** (Alias Tube/Fillet Flange, ICEM Surf Flanges and Fillet+) make the roll and the straight flange in one operation. The flange is set by Sweep Angle (default 90 degrees) or by a Parting Line pull vector with draft, plus a length, and **only one of radius, flange length or flange angle may vary** along the rail ([Alias 2024](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-6C6C9216-B6EB-4FAF-B8DA-D62CB5E5831B.html); [3DS community](https://3dswym.3dexperience.3ds.com/post/catia-creative-design-styling-user-community/icem-surf-tips-tricks-parametric-fillets-and-flanges_VUlQhlj9Q0G-CTYLnKj0xA)). Choosing G2 in Tube Flange removes the 360-degree tube, so the roll becomes a curvature-matched blend rather than a true arc ([Alias 2024: Tube Flange](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-71AD4A98-9709-4696-97F6-551DE7BB72D5.html)). **Build-and-blend** (eyebrow, gap, Free Form Fillet, or Rhino BlendSrf then MatchSrf) is the method for a true flare. Rhino users bulge the originals out first so the blend has less to do, and use position continuity on the wheel side ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/smooth-transition-to-wheel-arch-how/205775)). **Radial section sweeps** suit a lip whose section changes round the arch.

On continuity, Class-A work targets G2 and often G3 ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2019/ENU/Alias-Tutorials/files/GUID-64611955-D2CC-44F2-98F0-D4F1FE931D8B.htm)), but working modellers accept **G1 where the arch geometry makes curvature impossible** ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/srf-match-challenge-unsolved/98637)) and hide tangency-only breaks at shut lines ([Autodesk Alias help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/GUID-545A5B42-8EC5-479D-9739-6C4ACE21DE2B.htm)). The return is physically an **L-section inner lip** welded and sealed to the wheelhouse, which rolling turns into a tighter V or flat U ([Apex Wheels](https://apexwheels.com/blog/technical-discussion/tech-article-will-you-need-rolled-fenders)). Kunos models it cheaply: the shell's open edge turns inward and continues as a liner, with an extra loop at the lip and another at the turn-under (local `KN5_STUDY.md` §5). The recurring failure modes are too many CVs on the flange ([LearningAlias](https://blog.learningalias.co.uk/2010/02/24/autodesk-alias-building-a-car-body/)), crowded control points at tight bends ([McNeel](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/srf-match-challenge-unsolved/98637)), Sweep2 bulging as it rotates and scales the profile to reach a farther rail ([McNeel](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/sweep-2-rails-bulge/27728)), and twisted parameterisation that rotates highlights ([Novedge](https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/alias-patch-layout-mastery-5-class-a-strategies-for-clean-highlights-stable-edits-and-engineering-ready-surfaces)).

## Fit the strip in rail coordinates, then pin two pole rows

Reverse-engineering literature treats this region as a swept surface, not a rolling-ball blend. Benkő, Martin and Várady fit primary surfaces first and add blends afterwards, and reduce swept regions to curve fitting of projected points with continuity written as constraint equations ([ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/1811/1/REalgorithms.pdf); [constrained fitting](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/1812/1/ConstrainedFitting.pdf)). Constant-radius rolling-ball recovery is fully set by a radius once the primaries are known ([Kós, Martin, Várady 2000](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/13565)), but a flared, rolled lip has a section that changes round the arch. That makes Ueng and Lai's **sweep-surface fitting**, B-spline sections moved along a B-spline spine by nonlinear least squares, the closer model ([Ueng & Lai 1998](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/a-sweep-surface-fitting-algorithm-for-reverse-engineering/811161574281576448-2477)). For detection, the industrial approach fits circles to sections along maximum-curvature directions and flags a fillet when the fit error is low ([Justia patent 20240135733](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20240135733)), and a simpler variant cuts the mesh with a plane orthogonal to the fillet and fits a circle to the section ([koreascience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200532217917382.pdf)). Curvature-based detection fails when the neighbouring faces are not planar ([UEA](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/73650/2/Accepted_Manuscript.pdf)), and a car wing never is. So **rail-normal sections, fitted in 2D, are more robust than per-vertex curvature** on this kind of mesh.

The recommended pipeline in numpy + FreeCAD 1.1 has six steps. First, extract the opening boundary loop and fit an arc-length-parameterised rail with `Part.BSplineCurve().approximate(..., ParamType='ChordLength')` ([FreeCAD commit](https://gitea.suzanne.soy/suzanne.soy/FreeCAD/commit/f13f57ef3d2e1dbef8c3bfb47bda44759c1accdd)). Second, carry a **rotation-minimising frame by double reflection**, which has fourth-order global error against second order for the older projection and rotation methods and is defined on any C1 curve ([Wang et al. 2008](https://i.cs.hku.hk/GraphicsGroup/publications/pdf/Computation%20of%20rotation%20minimizing%20frames.pdf)). Frenet frames flip at inflections and are undefined on straight runs. For an arch, a better "across" vector is the panel normal projected perpendicular to the rail tangent, which is what OCC's SpineSupport mode does ([OCC refman](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_offset_a_p_i___make_pipe_shell.html)). With a near-circular arch, the plain radial direction from the arch centre, projected into the panel, is an even simpler frame and matches the Alias pivot. Third, express every mesh point as (s, t, h): arc length along the rail, distance across in the panel, and height off it. Fourth, solve a **least-squares tensor B-spline in (s,t)** with a second-difference smoothing term, and hand the poles to `Part.BSplineSurface().buildFromPolesMultsKnots(...)`. Fifth, for G1, take pole row 0 from the panel's boundary curve on the same s knot vector and constrain row 1 to row 0 plus a positive multiple of the panel's cross-boundary tangent. That is the standard sufficient condition, and it is the "constraint equations" route of Benkő et al. Sixth, because (s,t) are the surface's native (u,v), trims become isoparametric and `surf.segment(u0,u1,v0,v1)` cuts exactly with no Booleans.

OCC offers alternatives, and each has a catch. `GeomAPI_PointsToBSplineSurface` (FreeCAD `Part.BSplineSurface.approximate`) needs a regular grid and has no edge constraints ([OCC refman](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_geom_a_p_i___points_to_b_spline_surface.html)). `BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell` sweeps fitted sections along the rail, but G1 to the panel is not guaranteed. The FreeCAD wrapper has no `setLaw`, auxiliary-spine contact modes give **only C0 surfaces** ([OCC refman](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_offset_a_p_i___make_pipe_shell.html); [FreeCAD .pyi](https://huggingface.co/AbdulElahGwaith/FreeCAD/blob/main/src/Mod/Part/App/BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell.pyi)), and the auxiliary-spine wrapper has a filed bug ([FreeCAD tracker 2961](https://tracker.freecad.org/view.php?id=2961)). `Part.BRepOffsetAPI_MakeFilling` (GeomPlate) is the built-in way to get a patch that is **G1 to a support face and passes through scan points**. It takes edges with Order 0/1/2, point constraints and an initial surface, and reports `G0Error/G1Error/G2Error` ([FreeCAD .pyi](https://huggingface.co/AbdulElahGwaith/FreeCAD/blob/main/src/Mod/Part/App/BRepOffsetAPI_MakeFilling.pyi); [OCC BRepFill_Filling](https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/occt-7.8.0/refman/html/classBRepFill__Filling.html)). The best role for it is polishing: load the least-squares spline as the initial surface, add the junction edge at Order 1 against the panel face, and subsample to a few hundred points. It is also the tool for closing N-sided corners where the arch meets a swage or shut line. For run-outs at the sill or bumper cut, no source covers termination directly. Fade the section height to zero with a smoothstep over the last 20-50 mm as weighted pseudo-data, then trim on the free edge. MakePipeShell's vertex end-sections are the OCC analogue ([OCC refman](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_offset_a_p_i___make_pipe_shell.html)).

## Measured on the Kunos AE86 front wing

These are our own measurements, not from the research notes. The AE86 arch opening edge is close to a circle, centred **260 mm up and 1204 mm forward** in AC coordinates with a **355 mm radius**. The real edge lies within **-3 to +6 mm** of that circle. Kunos built the arch with **concentric vertex rings at about 0, 25, 65 and 100 mm** out from the edge. That matches the polygon consensus of full rings round the opening, and the first ring sits at the same 25 mm as the Alias flange width. The lip is a **~30 mm proud rim** that falls **~35 mm back to the body side over the next ~35 mm**, so the flare section is roughly a 45-degree ramp from rim to side, within about 70 mm of the edge. Fitting that band polar-style, as arc length round the arch x distance out from the real edge, gave **1.64 mm p95 on the 0-70 mm band**. The previous along-the-car band fit gave **13 mm**. In other words, a parameterisation that follows the opening is about eight times more accurate on the same data, because each fitted v-line now runs parallel to a Kunos ring rather than cutting across the lip. The near-perfect circle also makes the frame trivial: radial from (1204, 260) in side view, projected into the panel.

## Conclusion

The AE86 result shows that the parameterisation is the model. Class-A tutorials, Rhino forums and Kunos topology all describe the arch in the same polar terms (one centre, radial sections, concentric rings), and a fit that adopts those coordinates gets most of the accuracy before any constraint work starts. The next gains are structural, not statistical. Split the band at 12 o'clock into 2-3 spans with matching knot counts, pin pole rows 0-1 to the side panel for G1, add a separate inboard return as a one-rail sweep, and fade the band out at the sill and bumper cuts. Use GeomPlate only for corners and final polish. The open unknowns are the real lip crown radius, the return-flange depth and any taper of flare height round the arch. The literature does not give them, but the existing kn5 section tools can measure them directly from the 25/65/100 mm rings. Judge them by eye against the photos, as with the rest of the replica work.
