# Soft step / swage / raised-centre feature lines that fade out (Class-A bonnet surfacing)

Scope: how professional Class-A modellers build a low, soft step (1-3 mm high, 10-15 mm wide) between a raised centre panel and the outer bonnet panels, with the height fading along the line and the line curving in plan. Target case: AE86 bonnet, centre panel 1.7 mm above the outer panels at the rear, drop over 10-15 mm, fading to about 1.2 mm at the front; the line converges inward toward the front and hooks outward at the rear edge.

Overall source situation: vendor help pages (Autodesk Alias, CATIA) document the tools and their parameters well. I found NO public vendor or practitioner tutorial that walks through exactly "a soft step on a bonnet that fades out". ICEM Surf documentation is behind Dassault's login; only spec sheets are public. The construction method below is therefore assembled from tool documentation, one Class-A tutorial (Y-fillets), stamping research on "subtle feature lines", and reverse-engineering literature. Inferences are labelled.

## 1. How such a line is built: two primary surfaces plus a variable blend, or one surface plus a feature tool? Named tools and parameters

### Takeaway
The documented, tool-supported route in every Class-A package is "primary surfaces first, transition second": build the centre panel and the outer panel as separate primary surfaces (they are offset from each other by the step height) and join them with a variable-section fillet/blend whose width and shape vary along a guide curve. The packages have dedicated tools for this transition (Alias Surface Fillet with Variable Fillets and chord/tangent-length construction, Alias Freeform Blend/Profile Blend with G2/G3 sides, CATIA Styling Fillet with variable radius, ICEM Surf Advanced Fillet with "Two Chord lengths / 3 Radii / True minimum radius"). Gap/flange tools (Alias Panel Gap, ICEM Tube/Gap) are for shut lines, not soft steps.

### Cited Findings
- Class-A layout principle: "The layout of the surface data is the key to Class A modeling" (attributed to Barry Kimball); tolerances are set separately for "main blends, smaller fillets and detail fillets"; "there are no absolute rules for tolerances or continuity levels" — [Autodesk Alias tutorial: Understanding Class A Modeling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tutorials/files/GUID-64611955-D2CC-44F2-98F0-D4F1FE931D8B.htm)
- Standard Class-A practice for transitions is to build a parent (primary) surface, trim it, then build a secondary surface over it; the secondary is built with Birail "because it creates a uniform CV structure from A to B" and matched to the parent with the Match command — [Car Body Design, Y-Fillets in Class A Surfaces, D. Singh (Maruti Suzuki styling), p.3](https://www.carbodydesign.com/archive/2010/06/y-fillet-class-a-surfaces-tutorial/3/)
- Alias Surface Fillet parameters: Construction Type = Radius, Arc (Chord), Existing Theoretical, Build Theoretical (Radius sets the profile by radius; the others set the contact lines by width); Section Type = G0 Chamfer, G1 Circular, G2 Curvature, G3 Curvature, G2 Curvature/Arc; Curvature side = Both / Side 1 / Side 2; size = Radius/Center radius, Tangent offset, Chord length, Tangent length; Form Factor 0.1 ("sharper") to 2 ("flatter"); "Use minimum radius" check; Flow Control Auto/User Defined — [Alias 2024 Surface Fillet](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-05F7F4E3-8BCB-4423-A90D-0D4E8AA2FD9B.html)
- Alias Variable Fillets: shows a blue "fillet guide curve" (the theoretical intersection of the input surface sets) with a 0-1 parameter range; any number of size locators can be placed along it and the fillet blends between the values — [Alias 2014 Surface Fillet](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/SurfacesMultiSurfacefilletSurfacefillet.htm); [Alias 2024 Surface Fillet](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-05F7F4E3-8BCB-4423-A90D-0D4E8AA2FD9B.html)
- Alias G2 Curvature/Arc section "creates a fillet that has a short lead-in with curvature"; Lead-In sets the lead-in length; Auto Adjust Sliders sets Form Factor and Arc Range as Lead-In changes. - Alias version conflict: one help page says with Variable Fillets on, Center Radius becomes unavailable and Form Factor directly controls the centre radius; another says only tangent offsets (not centre radii) can be specified — check the version — [Alias 2014 Surface Fillet](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/SurfacesMultiSurfacefilletSurfacefillet.htm); [Alias 2012 Surface Fillet](https://download.autodesk.com/us/alias/2012help/files/SurfacesMultiSurfacefilletSurfacefillet.htm)
- Alias Freeform Blend / Profile Blend: G3 offered as Side 1/Side 2 continuity (since Alias Automotive/Surface 2011); G3 needs degree 6 for one side, degree 7 for both sides; shape set by Form Factor (0.1-2.0, needs both sides at least G1) or Shape ("looseness/tightness" toward a boundary); with both sides G0 a crown can be added — [Alias 2011 G3 in Freeform/Profile Blend](https://download.autodesk.com/us/alias/2011help/files/WS1a9193826455f5ff4e421d7d11bf108001d-489b.htm); [Alias 2022 Freeform Blend](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-C4A14175-8BED-4B74-856C-5704468E942C.html); [Alias 2022 Freeform Curve Blend](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Curve-Edit-palette/GUID-A8F5295D-FD68-4C33-914C-9E2578A37878.html)
- Alias Panel Gap (Surfaces > Rolled Edge) is a shut-line tool: Gap distance (shortest distance between primary and secondary rolled edges), Gap Position (Center line / Primary side), Primary/Secondary Section Type (G1 Circular, G1 Tangent, G2 Curvature), flanges by sweep angle or parting-line vector + draft, Variable geometry manipulators for gap width and flange vectors (ends constant, interior interpolated), Gap check deviation comb with threshold. Inputs must be G1 — [Alias 2024 Panel Gap](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-BDB7551E-A500-41DF-8A81-19A1DA71FFAA.html); [Alias 2015 Panel Gap](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2015/ENU/Alias-Reference/files/SurfacesRollededgePanelgap.htm)
- CATIA Styling Fillet (FreeStyle, needs FreeStyle Shaper licence): Continuity G0 or G2; Variable Radius toggle puts a radius manipulator at each end, "The radius varies linearly from one end of the fillet to the other"; dragging a manipulator inward adds intermediate radii and then "The radius varies non-linearly"; input edges must be G1 "in order to generate a continuous variable fillet"; extrapolation for non-intersecting surfaces — [CATIA help: Creating a Styling Fillet](https://catiahelp.azurewebsites.net/English/GsdUserMap/fss-t-SurfaceCreation-StylingFillet.htm)
- CATIA GSD variable-radius fillet allows R=0 values along a spine (licence-dependent) — [City University CATIA tutorial: Variable Radius Fillet](https://www.staff.city.ac.uk/~ra600/ME2105/Catia%20course/CATIA%20Tutorials/prtug_C2/prtugbt0609.htm) (as summarised by search; page not opened)
- ICEM Surf Advanced Tools add-on: "Advanced Fillet ... alternative fillet generation types such as Two Chord lengths, 3 Radii, True minimum radius which are associated with styling driven requirements"; "Tube ... create gaps with fillets based on a centre line and width defined in the viewing direction"; "Gap" for flanges/crimp flanges along a guide curve; "Accelerated Surfaces ... calculated based on a curve and a smooth transition to reference surfaces" (for spring-back compensation); Corner Blend for 3-surface patch blends — [ICEM Surf 415 Advanced Tools spec sheet](https://www.3ds.com/fileadmin/PRODUCTS-SERVICES/CATIA/PDF/ICEM_Surf_415_Advanced_Tools_Spec_Sheet_K76.pdf)
- ICEM Surf Master add-on covers "global filleting, Overcrowning, Feature Design and global connections of surfaces" (no public detail on Feature Design) — [ICEM Surf 413 Master spec sheet](https://www.3ds.com/fileadmin/PRODUCTS-SERVICES/CATIA/PDF/ICEM_Surf_413_Master_Spec_Sheet_K46.pdf) (as summarised by search)
- Car-design vocabulary: a feature line is "a strong section change involving two or more surface intersections", contrasted with a crease (simple intersection of two surfaces); synonyms swage line, bone line — [Car Design News glossary](https://www.cardesignnews.com/designers/a-is-fora-comprehensive-glossary-of-automotive-design-terms/506932)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For the AE86 step, the method that matches the documented tools is: (a) outer bonnet primary surface; (b) centre-panel primary surface as a separate surface that is roughly an offset of the outer one by a variable distance (1.7 mm rear -> 1.2 mm front), shaped with the same crown; (c) a plan-view guide curve for the line (converging forward, hooking out at the rear) used to trim both primaries leaving a 10-15 mm band; (d) a variable G2 (or G3) blend/fillet across that band. In Alias this would be Surface Fillet with Construction Type = Arc (Chord) or Build Theoretical and Variable Fillets locators for the band width, or a Freeform Blend between the two trim edges with G2/G3 sides; in CATIA, Styling Fillet (variable) or a Blend surface; in ICEM, Advanced Fillet with Two Chord lengths.
- (Inference) Because the two primaries do not intersect (they are parallel-ish, offset 1.2-1.7 mm), a radius-driven fillet has no theoretical intersection to roll on; chord/width-driven construction (fixed contact lines) or a boundary blend between trim edges is the natural choice. This is why "Build Theoretical"/"Arc (Chord)" and CATIA's extrapolation option exist.
- (Inference) Gap tools (Panel Gap, ICEM Tube/Gap) are the wrong family: they make a channel with walls/flanges, not a single S-step.

### Gaps
- No public ICEM Surf or CATIA ICEM Shape Design documentation of "Feature Design"/"feature line" tools; Dassault user docs need a login.
- No public tutorial found that builds exactly a raised-centre bonnet step; no Alias "Bead"/"Emboss" style tool was found in the docs searched.
- NX "Styled Blend"/law-controlled blend and Rhino BlendSrf/ Variable fillet not researched for lack of tool-call budget.

## 2. How a feature is made to VANISH (fade out) cleanly; continuity at each side and at the vanishing end

### Takeaway
Documented practice is: G2 (or G3 for highlight quality) along the long edges of the transition, but the run-out/termination points are allowed lower continuity (G0 at the end points, G1 locally where G2 cannot be held) and the surface is split where G2 fails. Fades are driven by a variable size law along a guide curve (Alias size locators, CATIA linear/non-linear radius manipulators, Rhino SubD crease weights going to 0 % at the ends).

### Cited Findings
- In the Y-fillet run-out, the secondary surface gets "G2 at edge C with the fewest CVs possible, and G0 at A and B" (the run-out points); G2 at the inflecting edge "You will not be able to achieve G2 condition at edge D", so the surface is split where G1 starts to fail and the parts matched separately; final surface G2 on three edges and G1 on one, and "The G1 continuity at edge 1 will fulfill the required level of quality and highlights" — [Car Body Design, Y-Fillets in Class A Surfaces p.3](https://www.carbodydesign.com/archive/2010/06/y-fillet-class-a-surfaces-tutorial/3/)
- Same tutorial: consecutive fillet surfaces can still give good highlights without full G2 because the U or V curvature changes as the fillet runs along the edges — [Car Body Design p.3](https://www.carbodydesign.com/archive/2010/06/y-fillet-class-a-surfaces-tutorial/3/)
- Class-A highlight quality: "G3 Continuity across the surface patches is often used to achieve this" — [Alias: Understanding Class A Modeling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tutorials/files/GUID-64611955-D2CC-44F2-98F0-D4F1FE931D8B.htm)
- Variable-size laws: Alias size locators interpolate between values along the 0-1 guide curve — [Alias 2014 Surface Fillet](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/SurfacesMultiSurfacefilletSurfacefillet.htm); CATIA linear (two end manipulators) or non-linear (intermediate manipulators) radius variation — [CATIA Styling Fillet help](https://catiahelp.azurewebsites.net/English/GsdUserMap/fss-t-SurfaceCreation-StylingFillet.htm); Alias Panel Gap keeps the end width constant and interpolates interior manipulators — [Alias 2024 Panel Gap](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-BDB7551E-A500-41DF-8A81-19A1DA71FFAA.html)
- Rhino SubD fading feature: select the edge range, SubDCrease gives 0 % weight at both ends and user weights (0-100 %) at interior points to create a gradient; weighted edges sit "somewhere between a smooth edge and a creased edge" and keep tangent and curvature continuity; moving the weighted edge "washes out or accentuates" the feature — [Rhino guide: Knife Handle, Create Fading Features](https://www.rhino3d.com/docs/guides/subd/subd-crease/)
- McNeel forum advice for fading SubD features: add edge loops and control proximity with SlideEdge rather than a single crease; partial edge loops can be added/removed so details fade in or out — [McNeel: crease division wish](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/wish-add-please-creasedivision-tangencyhandles-to-crease-control/98171); [McNeel: SubD challenge 4, fading hull chine](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/subd-challenge-4-fading-hull-chine/102164)
- A CATIA V5 video documents a "Blend Out Class A Method", described as a simple parametric technique to blend out into an adjacent surface (content not transcribed) — [YouTube: Blend Out Class A Method - Catia V5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FwYpQkLEgRo)

### Inferences
- (Inference) The AE86 line does NOT fully vanish (1.7 -> 1.2 mm, still about 70 % of its height at the front), so it is a tapered step, not a die-out. The cleaner construction is a variable offset law between the two primaries (height law along the line) with a near-constant band width, rather than a fillet whose width goes to zero. A true die-out (height -> 0) would need a G0 point or a degenerate/collapsed end on the transition surface, which the Y-fillet tutorial handles by G0 end points.
- (Inference) The height law should itself be smooth (linear or a gentle monotone curve in arc length). A linear law is what CATIA gives with two end values; an intermediate locator only if the photos show the fade is not linear.
- (Inference) Where the line hooks outward at the rear edge, plan-view curvature of the guide curve rises sharply; this is the place most likely to need a split of the transition surface (as in the Y-fillet tutorial) to keep G2 along the long edges.

### Gaps
- No primary source found giving a numeric rule for "how long" a run-out should be relative to the feature height or width.
- No ICEM/CATIA documentation found on degenerate-end blends for crease die-outs.

## 3. Typical cross-section of a soft step; radii versus height

### Takeaway
No source gives a published "standard" soft-step section. The tool documentation implies the section is a two-sided S: either two tangent circular arcs (G1 Circular) or a curvature-continuous G2/G3 S-blend (Form Factor / Lead-In tuned). Stamping research gives a hard lower bound: subtle feature lines with small direction changes need large radii (20 mm at 5 deg, 8 mm at 10 deg, 7 mm at 15 deg, 4 mm at 20 deg).

### Cited Findings
- A "subtle feature line" is "a narrow and long curved surface on a smooth panel"; experimental minimum formable radius was 20 mm, 8 mm, 7 mm and 4 mm for punch angles of 175, 170, 165 and 160 degrees (i.e. 5-20 deg direction change); FE, analytical and experimental agree — [Lee, Yu, Youn, Chung, Int. J. Automotive Technology 22(4) 993-1001, 2021, doi 10.1007/s12239-021-0089-0](https://pure.seoultech.ac.kr/en/publications/the-minimum-formable-radius-of-subtle-feature-lines-in-automotive/)
- Skid lines (stamping defect along character lines) come from sliding between the punch fillet and the sheet; a hood-panel case cut sliding distance from 10 to 3.5 mm — [SAGE 2026 paper, abstract via search](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1177/09544070261459230) (full text not accessed); a Japanese patent example uses a 3 mm character-line radius between panel faces at 85 and 65 deg to the press direction — [US patent 11813659 (press forming of automotive outer panel)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11813659) (as summarised by search)
- Alias G1 Circular + Form Factor 1.0 = exact circular arc; G2 Curvature/Arc = circular middle with short curvature lead-ins; Form Factor 0.1 sharper to 2 flatter — [Alias 2014 Form Factor note](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/GUID-73B0B42B-C556-477C-8A90-9523DDF4BEEA.htm); [Alias 2024 Surface Fillet](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-05F7F4E3-8BCB-4423-A90D-0D4E8AA2FD9B.html)

### Inferences (my own geometry, not from a source)
- Two equal tangent arcs (G1 S) of radius R turning angle t each, total width w and height h: w = 2R sin t, h = 2R(1 - cos t), so h/w = tan(t/2).
  - h 1.7, w 12 mm: t = 16.1 deg, R about 21.6 mm. h 1.7, w 10: t = 19.3 deg, R about 15.1 mm. h 1.7, w 15: t = 12.9 deg, R about 33.6 mm. h 1.2, w 12: t = 11.4 deg, R about 30.3 mm.
- G2 S-blend modelled as a quintic smoothstep y = h(6s^5 - 15s^4 + 10s^3), s = x/w (zero slope AND zero curvature at both edges, so G2 to flat neighbours): max slope = 1.875 h/w (about 15 deg for 1.7 over 12 mm), peak curvature about 5.77 h/w^2, i.e. minimum radius about w^2/(5.77 h) = 14.7 mm for 1.7 x 12 mm, about 20.8 mm for 1.2 x 12 mm. A cubic smoothstep (G1 only, curvature jumps at the edges) gives R_min = w^2/(6h), about 14.1 mm.
- So a 1.2-1.7 mm step over 10-15 mm implies section radii of roughly 10-35 mm and a direction change of about 11-20 deg at the inflection. That sits right in the stamping study's "subtle feature line" range (punch angle about 160-170 deg, formable radius 4-8 mm), so the AE86 numbers are physically plausible for a pressed steel bonnet.
- Because the step height falls toward the front while the width stays roughly constant, the section radii grow toward the front (about 22 -> 30 mm for w 12): the line looks softer at the front even if the band width is unchanged. If the photos show the line looking equally crisp front and rear, the band width probably narrows toward the front too (keep h/w roughly constant).

### Gaps
- No OEM or modeller source found giving typical radii for soft steps or swage lines on bonnets.
- No source found specifying whether OEMs use symmetric or asymmetric S-sections (e.g. tighter radius on the upper/centre side) for raised-centre bonnets.

## 4. Reverse-engineering workflows: fit panels first, then the blend? Accepted deviations

### Takeaway
Reverse-engineering literature and practice segment the mesh into large primary surfaces and transitional features (fillets, "free-form steps"), fit primaries first, then rebuild blends as small transition surfaces joining them. Scan-match tolerance is set per project by the OEM; no universal Class-A number is published. One case study cites +/-0.15 mm.

### Cited Findings
- Engineering parts are bounded by relatively large primary surfaces connected by highly curved transitional features such as fillets or free-form steps; blends must be reconstructed as small transition surfaces which smoothly join the primary surfaces (summary of reverse-engineering algorithm literature) — [Cardiff RE algorithms paper (Varady/Martin et al.)](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/1811/1/REalgorithms.pdf) (as summarised by search; not opened)
- Practice guidance: fit dominant primitives first, peel them off, iterate for smaller features; when a fit has acceptable mean but unacceptable max deviation concentrated in one area, the selection probably includes a blend or a scan artefact — [Novedge: Mesh-to-BRep](https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/mesh-to-brep-from-scans-to-intentful-editable-b-reps) (as summarised by search)
- Class-A reverse engineering: "The modeler will be given a tolerance by which the NURBS data must match the scan"; "scan data will always have some imperfection, or 'noise'"; perfect highlights are judged by a combination of continuity checks, cross-section editing and diagnostic shading, not one measure — [Alias: Understanding Class A Modeling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tutorials/files/GUID-64611955-D2CC-44F2-98F0-D4F1FE931D8B.htm)
- One industrial case study (not a standard) used an accuracy requirement of +/-0.15 mm with a deviation map, iterating until at least 97 % of main fitting points were inside spec, and a 0.08 mm global surface tolerance for one area — [2B Digital case study](https://2bdigital.sa/?p=23525) (as summarised by search)
- Geomagic Design X / PolyWorks: only marketing material found (deviation analysis, "exact surfacing" tier); nothing on fillet-specific workflows — [Oqton/Kindig-It case](https://oqton.com/posts/kindig-it-design-builds-cooler-cars-faster-with-oqtons-geomagic-control-x-and-design-x/); [MatterHackers Design X listing](https://www.matterhackers.com/store/l/geomagic-design-x-software/sk/MWDP1XQZ)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For the AE86 bonnet from a scan/mesh: fit the outer panel and the centre panel separately on mesh regions that exclude the 10-15 mm step band (plus a margin), measure the offset between the two fitted primaries along the line to get the real height law (check the 1.7 -> 1.2 mm), extract the line in plan from the mid-height of the step band, then build the blend between trimmed primaries and check it against the band's mesh. Fitting one surface across the step would force the fit to smear a 1-2 mm feature across many CVs and produce ripples.
- (Inference) With a 1.2-1.7 mm feature, a scan-match tolerance of 0.1-0.15 mm is about 10 % of the step height; the step band should be judged by section comparison (section curves through mesh and surface) more than by colour deviation maps.

### Gaps
- No Geomagic Design X, PolyWorks, CATIA DSE/QSR or Rhino RE documentation found that specifically covers fitting fillets/steps between auto-surfaced panels (not researched in depth for tool-call budget).
- No published Class-A scan tolerance norm; OEM-specific.

## 5. Highlight / zebra behaviour: what makes such lines look clean

### Takeaway
Sources agree that Class-A quality is judged by highlight/reflection behaviour, with G2 the baseline and G3 used for the best highlights; highlights must run smoothly and evenly along the line. No source gives a specific zebra rule for soft steps.

### Cited Findings
- "'Perfect Highlights' can't be judged by a single evaluation"; combination of surface continuity checks, cross-section editing and diagnostic shading; G3 across patches often used — [Alias: Understanding Class A Modeling](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tutorials/files/GUID-64611955-D2CC-44F2-98F0-D4F1FE931D8B.htm)
- Class-A described as mainly in G2/G3 curvatures for smooth reflections — [Wikipedia: Class A surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_A_surface)
- Feature lines, done well, give "subtle curvature and tension with good highlights"; crown (compound curvature) is how designers control highlights and lightlines — [Car Design News glossary](https://www.cardesignnews.com/designers/a-is-fora-comprehensive-glossary-of-automotive-design-terms/506932)
- Dassault notes the subtle interplay of light and shade over a bonnet strongly shows surface quality — [Dassault: ICEM Surf product page](https://www.3ds.com/products/catia/icem-surf) (as summarised by search)
- Fillets along a line can give good highlights without full G2 where the U/V curvature changes smoothly along the run — [Car Body Design Y-fillets p.3](https://www.carbodydesign.com/archive/2010/06/y-fillet-class-a-surfaces-tutorial/3/)
- Alias Panel Gap's "Gap check" shows a deviation comb against a threshold (green/red quills) — an example of the comb-based checking used on lines — [Alias 2024 Panel Gap](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Alias-Tool-Palette-Reference/files/Surfaces-palette/GUID-BDB7551E-A500-41DF-8A81-19A1DA71FFAA.html)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For a soft step, a clean zebra shows: stripes bend smoothly into and out of the band with no kink at either band edge (G1 kink shows as a stripe corner; G2 jump shows as a stripe width change at the edge), stripe offset across the band that changes smoothly as the height law falls 1.7 -> 1.2 mm, and no waviness along the line in plan. A cross-section curvature comb at several stations along the line should show the same S-shape scaled smoothly, never a change in sign count.
- (Inference) The hook at the rear edge is where highlight breaks are most likely; compare zebra in a view looking along the bonnet from the windscreen.

### Gaps
- No published zebra/highlight acceptance criteria specific to soft steps or swage lines were found.
- Academic sources on reflection/highlight lines (Klass 1980; Beier and Chen 1994) were not fetched in this pass.
