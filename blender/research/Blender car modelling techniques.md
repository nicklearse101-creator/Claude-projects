# Build car panels exactly through authored curves

Professional car artists do not get crisp panels by fitting and smoothing a dense mesh. They author a small all-quad cage whose edge loops run along the car's feature lines and panel borders, hold edges sharp with closely spaced support loops (or creases and weighted bevels that are applied before export), cut shut lines as real offset borders with turned-in returns, and judge the result by how reflection stripes flow across it. This matches your own finding that panel outlines plus crease lines pin a panel to about 0.65 mm median across 17 Kunos cars. The curves carry the information, and the surface between them should simply interpolate them. Blender has no CAD-style curve-network surfacing: no Gordon or Coons surfaces on trimmed NURBS, and no built-in loft node as of 5.2 LTS. Its fully scriptable built-ins (bmesh `bridge_loops` and `grid_fill`, Subdivision, Shrinkwrap, Boolean, Weighted Normal, Data Transfer) are still enough for an automated builder, especially if the builder evaluates its own Coons/Gordon patches in NumPy and writes the grids straight into BMesh. Accuracy comes before any of this. Blueprints from the web are mostly not manufacturer drawings and their views disagree, so the professional order is a dimension table first, then wheels and wheelbase, then blueprints scaled to that frame, then photos matched with fSpy that settle any conflict. Most of the pipeline can be automated. The parts that resist automation are choosing the feature curves themselves, placing poles well on 3- and 5-sided regions, and the final judgement by eye against photos.

## Accuracy starts with a dimension frame, not the blueprint

Studios rank their sources: CAD first, then scans, then measured photos, then blueprints. Kunos said in 2019 that it was "in a lucky position to be provided with CAD data for the majority of the cars" in ACC, though photos are still needed, especially for interiors ([Unreal Engine interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/assetto-corsa-competizione-leans-into-realism-to-create-the-ultimate-racing-sim)). For older cars there is no data, so Kunos "usually use[s] blueprints and a lot of pictures" and is starting to use laser scans for measurements ([The Apex: Marco Massarutto](https://apex.custodian.club/marco-massarutto/)). A modder rebuilding a 1980s hatchback is in exactly that classic-car position. iRacing combines laser scans, manufacturer CAD and "thousands of digital photographs", says there is "no easy button", and builds every model by hand ([iRacing archive](https://iracingolder.racesimcentral.net/technology_modeling.htm)). Polyphony spends about **270 days per GT7 car**, using photography, laser and drone scans, colorimetry and manufacturer CAD ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/one-gran-turismo-7-car-takes-270-days-to-make)). Even studios that have CAD or scans rebuild the game mesh by hand and use the scan only as a dimension check, because editing a scan directly gives unusable topology ([3D Scan Store](https://3dscanstore.com/blog/3d-scanning-cars)). For a builder, this means reference accuracy and surface construction are separate stages, and the scan, AI mesh or blueprint is never the output topology.

In Blender, each blueprint view goes in as an Image > Reference empty. These empties have Depth, Side, Opacity and ortho-only display settings ([Blender Manual: Empties](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/modeling/empties.html)), and a car needs front, side, rear and top views as separate images ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/2/blender-blueprints-simply-explained)). These views are untrustworthy. Modellers report that most online blueprints "were not created by car companies", that a door measures differently in the top and side views, and that some prints were scanned skewed ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2007077/)). The defence is to scale every view to published wheelbase, track, length, width and height for the exact variant and year, and to note that "the size of the tire will set the scale" ([LDraw wiki: Scaling Down](https://wiki.ldraw.org/wiki/Scaling_Down)). A tyre code gives the wheel diameter directly: 175/65R13 is about 557 mm. A builder can run this check without help. Wheel ellipses in the side view should be circles, the side and top views should give the same length, and any view that will not agree gets logged rather than averaged. Your Nova project's 30 mm frame error, which only showed up against solved-camera photos, is the textbook case.

fSpy is an open-source standalone camera matcher that replaced BLAM and has an official Blender importer ([fSpy README](https://github.com/stuffmatic/fSpy/blob/develop/README.md)). It solves focal length and orientation from two perpendicular vanishing directions. It assumes the principal point is at the image centre, which asymmetric cropping breaks. Lens distortion and lines that are not truly perpendicular add error: the official tutorial's own example estimated **26 mm against a true 24 mm**, about 8% ([fSpy tutorial](https://fspy.io/tutorial/)). Common mistakes are setting more vanishing points than the image really has, and "fixing" perspective by dragging vanishing points when the principal point is the real problem ([Pampuch fSpy tutorial](https://blenderartists.org/t/complete-fspy-tutorial-all-options-explained/1394133)). Some cropped or warped stock images are "just plain impossible to match" ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/camera-matching-more-than-fspy/1243935)). Car bodies have few straight lines, so solve from the ground (bay lines, kerbs, buildings) and prefer long-lens, uncropped originals with EXIF data. Then refine by hand until the already-placed wheels and ground plane line up. Nobody publishes a mm-level accuracy figure for fSpy at car scale, and nobody publishes a modder tolerance checklist. So a solved camera should be treated as globally right and possibly locally wrong, and the overlay judged area by area.

## A panel is a patch bounded by its feature curves

Your 0.65 mm result has a firm theoretical basis. Transfinite interpolation, from Gordon and Hall in 1973, builds a surface, and directly a structured quad mesh, from its boundary curves alone ([Wikipedia: Transfinite interpolation](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfinite_interpolation)). A Coons patch is the four-boundary case. A Gordon surface extends it to a whole network of profile and guide curves, by applying the scheme to every curve and subtracting the tensor-product term (a Boolean sum) ([Bajaj et al., UT Austin](https://cs.utexas.edu/~bajaj/papers/1997/conference/crc-chap.pdf)). OpenCASCADE's `GeomFill_Gordon` is a working reference implementation ([OCCT](https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/refman/html/class_geom_fill___gordon.html)). A 2023 paper notes that no open-source project could at that time build a mesh from a set of Coons patches ([GraphiCon 2023](https://www.graphicon.ru/html/2023/papers/paper_093.pdf)), so writing your own is not reinventing a freely available wheel. Graphics research explains why curve-built patches read as designed. Bessmeltsev, Wang, Sheffer and Singh segment each closed 3D curve cycle into matching pairs of opposite segments and run the quad rows along the implied flow lines, on the premise that viewers complete a shape by imagining smooth flow lines between similar segments ([Design-driven quadrangulation, TOG 2012](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2012/Quadrangulation)). FlowRep runs the other way. It extracts compact flow-line curve networks from meshes of free-form man-made shapes ([FlowRep, TOG 2017](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2017/FlowRep/)), which is the right model for turning a scan or AI mesh into a curve cage.

Blender itself offers three routes. Native NURBS surfaces are untrimmed U/V control grids with no lofting or trimming tools ([Blender Manual: Surfaces](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/surfaces/introduction.html)). A replacement surface data-block has only been proposed ([GSoC 2025 draft](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/gsoc-2025-draft-proposal-surface-data-block/39884)), and 5.2 added only Set NURBS Order and Set NURBS Weight nodes on the curve side ([5.2 notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.2/geometry_nodes/)). A native "Bridge Curves" node exists only as a WIP pull request ([PR #114014](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/114014)). Add-ons fill the gap for human artists. Curves to Mesh turns four-curve networks into quad surfaces, and also offers Profiles (loft) and Sweep modes, but its Python entry points are undocumented ([docs](https://curves-to-mesh-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)). Loft Curves and Loft Tools are geometry-nodes lofts ([Loft Curves](https://superhivemarket.com/products/loft-curves/faq), [Loft Tools](https://dan-oak.github.io/blender-addon-loft-tools-docs)). The Higgsas "Splines Patch" node makes a grid from four splines ([Higgsas](https://higgsas-geo-nodes-manual.readthedocs.io/en/latest/curves.html)). Bsurfaces is stroke-driven, has had no release since February 2025 and has crash reports in 4.3 ([Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/bsurfaces-gpl-edition/versions)). The mesh-native route is the most controllable. Bridge Edge Loops with **Blend Surface interpolation** takes neighbouring surface normals into account, so a bridged span stays tangent to the panels on either side. Smoothness 1 uses the full Bezier, and Profile Factor inflates or pinches the span, which suits an arch flare but distorts flat skins ([Manual: Bridge Edge Loops](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/editing/edge/bridge_edge_loops.html)). Grid Fill "gives the best results if each pair of opposing sides has the same number of vertices", and its Simple Blending option is for flat areas ([Manual: Grid Fill](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/editing/face/grid_fill.html)).

For an automated builder, the cleanest design is to skip operator-based filling for the main skins. Instead, evaluate a bilinearly blended Coons patch per four-sided panel, `S(u,v) = (1-v)C0(u) + vC1(u) + (1-u)D0(v) + uD1(v) - bilinear(corners)`, with arc-length-resampled boundaries so the quads come out even. Promote it to a Gordon surface when an interior section curve exists, such as a mid-door section or the crown line of a bonnet. Then write the grid into BMesh as quads. Neighbouring panels built on the same boundary curve share their vertices exactly, so they weld cleanly and reflections line up. The surface passes through your crease lines by construction, instead of being pulled towards them by a smoothing energy. That is why curve-built panels come out crisp where fitted meshes come out lumpy. The code is my construction from the cited definitions, not a published Blender implementation. Three- and five-sided regions, such as arch corners and A-pillar bases, need splitting into quads at mid-edge points, as in Takayama-style sketch-based patch filling ([Disney Research, SIGGRAPH 2013](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Sketch-Based-Generation-and-Editing-of-Quad-Meshes-Paper.pdf)). This is the hardest geometric case to automate well.

## Sharpness lives in support-loop spacing and pole placement

The topology rules come from Pixar's OpenSubdiv notes. A good Catmull-Clark cage is mostly regular quads, manifold, and has "few extraordinary vertices". About six spans are enough for an accurate circle. Loop counts change through sparing valence-5 poles or pentagons, and a pole surrounded by triangles produces "wavy" surfaces ([OpenSubdiv modeling notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/release/documentation/mod_notes.rst)). Car practitioners add that modelling the whole body as one continuous quad mesh adds needless complexity. Panel lines are where loops are cut off and where density can rise locally ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2771704)). Detailing a blocked-out volume drags loops into areas that do not need them ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/breaking-edge-loops-around-subdiv-details/688905)). For a builder, the rules are these. Every cage face is a quad. Each panel is one patch whose boundary loops are its shut lines. Loop direction follows the main curvature: lengthwise on the bonnet and roof, around the radius at wheel arches. Poles go only on panel borders, at feature junctions or in hidden areas, never in a door skin, bonnet centre or roof. Separate panels are the strongest argument for automation, because they give every loop a natural place to end.

Edge sharpness under Catmull-Clark comes from how close the holding loop sits to the edge: closer is sharper, wider is softer, and loops jammed too close pinch ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2774947); [Tripo blog](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/smart-mesh-supporting-edges-for-crisp-corners)). The common rule is one support edge either side of a 90-degree corner. Blender's Subdivision modifier also reads edge creases, which make an edge "more resistant to the smoothing effect". It offers Use Limit Surface for the smoothest shape and Use Custom Normals to carry custom split normals through subdivision ([Manual: Subdivision Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/subdivision_surface.html)). OpenSubdiv calls semi-sharp creases cheaper than extra loops, with values above 5 rarely needed. Many export formats do not carry crease data, though, so game-bound models need real geometry ([Rhino SubD tip](https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/rhino-3d-tip-controlling-subd-edge-sharpness-with-creases-and-corners)). The practical answer for an AC builder is to author with creases and bevel weights, then apply Subdivision before FBX export so the sharpness is baked in. No source gives a numeric ratio between loop offset and fillet radius. As starting values I would use about 1-3 mm offsets for crisp feature lines and 5-15 mm for soft shoulders, then calibrate them with an automated sweep that measures curvature.

Shut lines are real geometry, not texture. Factory gaps run about **3.0 mm headlamp-to-wing and 5.0 mm rear door to body** on a Freelander ([lrman](https://www.lrman.ru/en/freelander/1/carcass/geometry/size_of_gaps_and_profiles)), and metrology targets are 3.5-4.5 mm for front door to fender ([iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/ai-vision-camera/ai-vision-gap-flush-measurement-automotive-body-assemblies)). Artists either split the panel border, move it back to open the gap and extrude the edge inward, or use a modifier stack: sharp-marked gap edges in a vertex group, then Edge Split, Solidify and a Bevel limited to that group. Shape-sharp edges elsewhere confuse Solidify unless it gets its own vertex group too ([Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/10/adventures-making-doors-in-blender.html)). Autodesk Alias's Panel Gap tool defines the gap as the shortest distance between two rolled flanges ([Alias help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Alias/files/SurfacesRollededgePanelgap.htm)), which is the right parameter for a script too. BeamNG requires detachable parts to come away "without creating holes", which means the return must be modelled ([BeamNG docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/vehicle-art/modeling/)). The automatable order is: build one continuous skin, split it along the shut-line loops, offset each border back by half the gap, extrude a 10-20 mm return straight inward, add one holding loop about 1 mm from the outer lip, then subdivide. Return depth and lip offset are my inference, because no CG source publishes standards for them. This keeps the panel surfaces exactly continuous across the gap.

## Lumps come from the cage, and stripes reveal them

Waviness is a symptom of the cage, not of the subdivision algorithm. Sources name irregular loop spacing, which shows as ripples ([Tripo blog](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/hd-model-avoiding-pinching-and-waviness-in-subd)); poles ringed by triangles ([OpenSubdiv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/release/documentation/mod_notes.rst)); support edges too close together; too few loops for the shape to hold ([Tuts+ Willys Jeep](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/modeling-a-high-poly-world-war-ii-willys-jeep-in-modo-part-2--cg-17445)); and cuts made on already-subdivided geometry, where "almost every cut and new triangle" shows in reflections ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2425349)). Each extra control vertex is another chance to sit off the intended surface. That explains your smoothing-fit failure. Fitting a dense mesh to noisy targets and then smoothing it leaves many slightly misplaced vertices, and a smoothing energy does not know where the designer's lines are. A curve-interpolated patch has few free vertices and none that are unconstrained by a curve.

The standard fixes still have a place as finishing passes. Shrinkwrap moves each vertex to the nearest point on a target, which fixes fit but not flow ([Manual: Shrinkwrap](https://docs.blender.org/manual/nb/4.5/modeling/modifiers/deform/shrinkwrap.html)). Blender Studio's geometry-nodes "relax_mesh_around_pins" relaxes a mesh while pinned edges and boundaries hold, and Julien Kaspar warns that leaving it live causes jitter, so it should run as a one-off step ([Blender Studio](https://studio.blender.org/blog/retopology-modifiers-pinning-relaxing)). In a builder, pin the feature curves and shut lines, relax only the interior, and run Shrinkwrap only against a smooth reference. A photogrammetry or AI target puts its own noise back into the panel, so re-measure after every Shrinkwrap.

Car modellers check surfaces with zebra-style stripe matcaps. The reflection bands must flow continuously and evenly across a panel and across its shut lines. The bundled stripe matcaps alias at 512x512, and users have asked for denser stripes ([devtalk: MatCaps](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/call-for-content-matcaps/737?page=17)). BeamNG bans normal maps on body panels because they "create unwanted reflection artifacts", which makes reflections the acceptance test ([BeamNG docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/vehicle-art/modeling/)). Every check can be scripted, so a builder can score its own output without a person. It can measure the ratio of adjacent edge lengths along each loop (flag above about 1.5), the non-planarity of quads on nominally flat panels, poles more than one ring inside a panel border, curvature sign flips across a convex panel (a lump), and deviation from a reference surface. It can also render fixed views with a procedural stripe world, from the sine of the reflection vector, and test whether the extracted stripe edges are smooth curves. These metrics and thresholds are my proposals and none is a published standard. Blender's Mesh Analysis overlay (Distortion, Intersect) does the same checks interactively, though I did not re-verify that in the manual.

## Openings, normals and the 125k budget decide the export

For holes in curved panels, artists cut with the Boolean modifier, often through the free Bool Tool, which was updated for 5.2 LTS in July 2026 ([Extensions: Bool Tool](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/bool-tool/)). They then repair shading. Weighted Normal "works almost always" ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/fix-shading-after-boolean/1241172)). Data Transfer of custom normals from an uncut duplicate is the standard fix for booleans on smooth curved surfaces ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2018/11/08/fixing-normals-on-curved-surfaces-with-holes/)). It fails when the cut vertices drift off the true curve, and users question how it behaves since Auto Smooth was removed ([CG Cookie](https://cgcookie.com/community/18228-normals-fix-did-it-change-on-4x)). Booleans leave n-gons and thin triangles on a subdivided panel. So for wheel arches and window apertures, a curve-driven builder should make the opening a boundary curve of the patch itself. Booleans are better kept for small vents and inserts on panels that will not be subdivided after the cut.

Sim-racing car bodies are "mid-poly": the cage with its support loops is subdivided once and kept as real geometry, and the cars are not baked high-to-low, because their surfaces are mostly flat with gentle curvature shifts ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2425349)). The Weighted Normal modifier offers Face Area, Corner Angle and combined modes. Weight 50 gives uniform weighting, and Keep Sharp preserves marked edges ([Manual: Weighted Normal](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/normals/weighted_normal.html)). BeamNG triangulates every object with Keep Normals itself, because export triangulation "messes up normals" ([BeamNG docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/vehicle-art/modeling/)). The most-cited AC budget comes from a Kunos artist: **LOD A exterior 125k tris, B 20-25k, C 10-12k, D 2-3k**, a 125k cockpit, wheels 5-7k (up to 12k), and 80-sided tyres. The guide also sets the conventions: metres, Y-up, wheels touching Y=0, CAPS dummy names, a material on every mesh, and FBX 2014/2015 ([assettocorsamods.net guide](https://assettocorsamods.net/threads/your-first-car-in-assetto-corsa-basic-guide.1019/)). A subdivision level of 1 on a 10-25k-quad cage gives roughly 80-200k tris, which matches that range. Forum reports show draw calls and textures matter as much as triangles: a 320k car ran well while a 330k car with four LODs struggled ([RaceDepartment](https://www.racedepartment.com/threads/drm-revival-paid.149302/page-23)).

## How much bpy can automate, and where it breaks

Nearly everything above is scriptable with built-ins. Geometry belongs in BMesh: it needs no UI context and returns the geometry it creates. BMesh faces are "most likely the easiest way to create faces in new scripts" ([Gotchas: Mesh Access](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/info_gotchas_meshes.html)). The relevant `bmesh.ops` are `bridge_loops`, `grid_fill`, `edgenet_fill`, `holes_fill`, `subdivide_edges`, `bevel`, `extrude_edge_only`, `spin`, `remove_doubles`, `triangulate`, `dissolve_limit` and `recalc_face_normals`. The docs' own example notes that some op return values are incomplete, so the builder should track its own geometry ([bmesh.ops 5.2](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bmesh.ops.html)). Scripts written for 3.x break in three ways. Dict context overrides were removed in 4.0 in favour of `context.temp_override(...)` ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/overriding-context-in-blender-4/1485970.md)). Creases and bevel weights became the generic attributes `crease_edge` and `bevel_weight_edge` ([4.0 Python notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.0/python_api/)). And `use_auto_smooth` gave way to a pinned "Smooth by Angle" modifier in 4.1 ([Plumber #184](https://www.github.com/lasa01/Plumber/issues/184)). Edit-mode data is written back only when you leave edit mode ([Gotchas](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/info_gotchas_meshes.html)). Headless builds run as `blender -b --factory-startup -P build.py -- args` ([CLI manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/nb/dev/advanced/command_line/arguments.html)). Bulk `foreach_set` only matters for dense evaluated meshes, not for a cage of a few thousand quads ([PR #114262](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/114262)). Several `bpy.ops` signatures in my notes were taken from memory, not from fetched docs, so check them with `dir()` in the running Blender.

| Stage | Automatable? | Built-in route | Human or add-on needed for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dimension frame, wheel placement | Fully | Script from spec table and tyre code | Sourcing correct variant data |
| Blueprint scaling and cross-view check | Fully | Reference empties plus scripted measurements | Deciding which conflicting view is right |
| Camera solve | Partly | fSpy import; scripted wheel/ground refinement | Line picking, judging the overlay by eye |
| Feature curves (outlines, creases) | Partly | Trace from photos or blueprints, or extract from a reference mesh (FlowRep-style) | Choosing which lines matter |
| Panel surfaces | Fully | NumPy Coons/Gordon into BMesh; `bridge_loops`/`grid_fill` | 3- and 5-sided region splits |
| Sharpness | Fully | `crease_edge`, `bevel_weight_edge`, Subdivision | Calibrating offsets |
| Shut lines and returns | Fully | Border offset plus extrude in BMesh | None |
| Openings | Fully | Patch boundaries; Boolean plus Data Transfer | Messy small inserts |
| Retopology of scan/AI references | Partly | QuadriFlow, Shrinkwrap a designed cage | Quad Remesher (paid), RetopoFlow (modal, not scriptable) |
| Quality scoring | Fully | Edge-ratio, planarity, curvature, stripe renders | Final sign-off against photos |
| Export | Fully | Apply stack via the depsgraph, Weighted Normal, Triangulate | Checking shading in ksEditor/CM |

The add-on market barely helps automation. Curves to Mesh, Hard Ops/Boxcutter, MESHmachine and RetopoFlow are interactive tools for people. QuadriFlow is built in and deterministic but "does not place clean edge loops" ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/best-blender-retopology-addons-2026)). Quad Remesher is the paid automatic standard, a Python bridge to a closed engine ([Exoside](https://exoside.com/quadremesher/)). Hardcraft (free, alpha, Blender 5.0+) and PatchGAP (paid, 5.2) turn CAD-export faces into editable patches ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/try-this-free-cad-data-retopology-tool-for-blender/)), the closest relatives to a patch-based builder. No open-source realistic car-body generator for Blender turned up. Existing generators are stylised or low-poly geometry-nodes products ([Vehicle Forge](https://superhivemarket.com/products/vehicle-forge/docs)). Industry parameterises car bodies differently. DrivAerNet morphs a CAD baseline in ANSA using 50 parameters on 32 morphable entities ([arXiv 2403.08055](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08055v1)), and DrivAerNet++ morphs over 8,000 designs with boxes for length, width and roof height ([arXiv 2406.09624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09624)). AI image-to-3D tools extract surfaces from an implicit field and produce dense triangles with no edge flow ([Neural4D](https://blog.neural4d.com/user-guide/blender-retopology-ai-3d-models/), a vendor source). No independent car-specific accuracy benchmark exists, so AI meshes belong only as Shrinkwrap targets or sources for curve extraction.

## Conclusion

Your finding changes the frame of the problem. A car body is a curve network with interpolated skins, so the builder's real product is the curve set. Surfacing becomes a deterministic, nearly solved step (Coons/Gordon into BMesh, creases, offset-and-return shut lines, one subdivision level), and almost all remaining effort and error sit upstream in getting the curves right: dimension frame, blueprint cross-checks, camera solves, and judging the overlay by eye. That points to an architecture the add-on ecosystem does not offer. It would hold a curve-network data model per car, from which panels, gaps, openings and LODs are all regenerated. Editing a curve then rebuilds every patch that touches it, the same "baseline plus parameters" pattern DrivAerNet uses but with designer lines as the parameters.

The open risks are clear and testable. They are poles in 3- and 5-sided regions, which is where lumps will most likely come back; the offset-to-radius calibration for support loops; and whether Data Transfer and custom normals survive the FBX-to-kn5 path. Each can be measured with the builder's own curvature and stripe metrics on the 17 Kunos cars you already hold. That measurement is also the strongest validation available, because no published tolerance standard exists to compare against.
