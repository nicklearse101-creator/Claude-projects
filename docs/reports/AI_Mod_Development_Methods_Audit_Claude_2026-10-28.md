## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

I have worked on **three Assetto Corsa car projects** and **two supporting tools** on this machine. I have done
**no BeamNG.drive work at all** — not a single JBeam, DAE or BeamNG package. The only BeamNG content anywhere
in my accessible material is a set of *citations* of a BeamNG modder's published Blender workflow, gathered as
research for an Assetto Corsa problem. Any comparison with an assistant that has done real BeamNG work should
treat my BeamNG column as empty, not weak.

The three car projects divide sharply by method, and that division is the single most useful finding in this
audit:

| Project | What it is | Dominant method | In-game status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Talbot Sunbeam Lotus rally** | rally conversion of an existing third-party mod | **procedural parts grafted onto someone else's mesh**, whole build scripted and repeatable | drivable, 7 physics versions, each kept as a backup car |
| **Ford Escort Mk2 DTM** | "90s DTM" conversion of an existing third-party mod | same pattern: scripted aero kit + scripted physics derived from a Kunos donor | drivable, v26 live, 8 backup cars |
| **Vauxhall Nova GTE 1989** | **a car modelled from nothing**, no donor mesh | measurement-first: photogrammetry + traced factory drawings, every panel generated by script | drove once in Phase 1 (block-out); the current body has **never been in the game** |

The two conversion projects work. They are end-to-end scripted, reproducible, versioned, and the cars load and
drive. The from-scratch project is where almost every hard failure in this audit lives, and where the
interesting methodology is.

Four findings I would put in front of another assistant:

1. **A measurement frame error can silently invalidate months of comparison.** On the Nova, everything traced
   off a blueprint sat **30.5 mm** along the car from everything measured off a photograph, because the two
   used different axle origins. Neither source was wrong; they simply disagreed about where zero was. Found
   2026-10-27, after it had been corrupting panel positions for weeks.
2. **A stale solver output is worse than no solver output.** The Nova's bundle-adjustment result file was not
   the solution of its own observation file — it predated the last edit of the marks. Re-solving moved the
   windscreen corners 30 mm. The user had been telling me by eye that the screen was too high; he was right and
   my numbers were wrong.
3. **The single biggest determinant of whether a panel "looks like a car" is whether its features are rows of a
   swept section, or cuts made across a finished grid.** The one part of the Nova that consistently looks right
   — the front bumper and grille — is a forty-row cross-section swept along a measured path, with returns at
   both ends. Everything that looks like paper was a single skin with holes cut through it afterwards.
4. **Generating a part is cheap; generating it *into the right place against another part* is the hard bit.**
   The current open fault on the Nova is a panel gap that should be 4 mm and is 7–10 mm, opening to ~40 mm,
   because two adjacent panels are each built to their own idea of the shut line rather than sharing one curve.

Honest self-criticism, up front: I spent far too long on the Nova improving measurement precision while the
model still had no mirrors, no wheels and panels that read as cardboard. Precision was not the binding
constraint; construction method was. I also repeatedly declared areas "fixed" on the strength of a low-resolution
overlay, and the user had to correct me — several times — by simply looking at the picture.

### 3.1 Vauxhall Nova GTE 1989 — modelled from nothing

Target: Assetto Corsa, Custom Shaders Patch, Content Manager. Car id `vauxhall_nova_gte_1989`.
No donor mesh. Rule in `CLAUDE.md`: *"No ripped or borrowed meshes from other games/mods."*
AI image-to-3D output was permitted as a starting point from 2026-10-13; that route was taken (Meshy) and then
**abandoned and deleted** on 2026-10-26 at the user's instruction, because the generated shell's lumps were
propagating into every panel built against it.

**Measurement sources.** Two independent systems, which is both the strength and the source of the worst bug:

- *Photogrammetry* (`tools/photomatch/`). Seven photographs of three real GTEs. 34 points triangulated by
  bundle adjustment: lamp corners, indicator corners, grille corners, door handle, side repeater, every window
  corner, screen and hatch glass corners. Anchors: wheelbase 2343 mm, tracks 1320/1307 mm, number plates

  line blueprint for the side elevation, plus a photographed **factory GA sheet** rectified by homography
  (`rectify_ga.py`) to recover the plan and front elevations. Output `blender/data/*.json`.

**Frame.** Car faces −Y, Z up, +X is the car's left, origin on the ground midway between the axles
(y = ±1.1715), metres. Photo-frame z sits on loaded tyres, the model on unloaded ones, so
**model z = photo z + 0.0193 m**.

**Build chain** — `sh tools/gen/rebuild_body.sh`, twelve headless Blender steps:

```
rebuild_sill → rebuild_bonnet_drawn → rebuild_scuttle_drawn → rebuild_wing_drawn
  → add_rear_end → build_shell2 → fit_shell → cut_apertures2
  → roll_edges → shut_lines → add_glass → organise
```

Output `blender/nova_proc.blend`, 37 parts in collection `NOVA_NEW`.

**Produced files.** `blender/data/*.json` (13 generated part/profile files), `blender/nova_proc.blend`,
`car/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/{*.kn5, collider.kn5, data/, ui/, skins/, sfx/, extension/, animations/}`,
deployed to `D:\SteamLibrary\...\content\cars\vauxhall_nova_gte_1989` (present, FILE-VERIFIED).

### 3.3 Ford Escort Mk2 DTM — conversion, assertion-checked physics

Target: AC. Car id `ford_escort_mk2_tuned`. Model by 101Creative, physics base Kunos/graveltrap, personal use.
v19 was undriveable; v20 rebuilt the chassis against Kunos' Mercedes 190E Evo II DTM; **v26 rebuilt it again on

Two notable practices here, both of which I would carry everywhere:

- **The build asserts its own consistency.** `mk1_build.py` "asserts that every setup default equals the
  physics value it adjusts" and that every LUT referenced actually exists. A physics build that cannot lie
  about its own setup defaults is a meaningfully better artefact than one that can.
- **Model and physics install separately.** `install.py install` copies physics only; `--with-model` is
  required to touch the kn5s, *because the user edits the model himself in Blender* and a physics-only install
  must not overwrite his work. This rule exists because of a real loss (see F008).

FILE-VERIFIED: backup cars v19–v25 plus `backup_pre_rally` present in `content/cars`.

## 4. BLENDER MODELLING AND AUTOMATION METHODS

Blender 5.2.1 LTS throughout. Two execution modes, used for different purposes:

- **Headless** — `blender -b <blend> --python <script> -- <args>`. Every Nova build step. Deterministic,
  scriptable, no GUI state to go wrong.
- **Live, through the Blender MCP add-on** — used on the Sunbeam and Escort, where the user also edits the
  blend by hand and the blend is therefore the master copy. `execute_blender_code` runs Python in his running
  Blender; `look` returns a viewport image.

### 4.1 What I actually use

Overwhelmingly **BMesh and NumPy**, not the interactive mesh operators. I build vertex grids from measured
functions and make faces directly:

```python
for i, y in enumerate(ys):                 # stations along the car
    col = [bm.verts.new((x, y, z)) for x, z in section(i)]
    V.append(col)
for i in range(len(ys) - 1):
    for j in range(NR - 1):
        f = bm.faces.new((V[i][j], V[i][j+1], V[i+1][j+1], V[i+1][j]))
        f[L_SEG] = SEG_OF_ROW[j + 1]       # which feature band this face belongs to
```

Modifiers I do use: **Mirror** (every panel is built left-only, `use_clip` on the centreline), **Solidify**
(sheet parts such as the bumper shell), occasionally **Bevel**. `BVHTree` is used heavily — for culling one
panel's faces where another covers them, for deciding whether a panel edge butts another panel, and for
projecting traced lines onto a surface.

**Boolean is deliberately not used** on the Nova. It was tried and abandoned: an exact boolean needs a closed
solid, and on an open one-skin shell it invented cap faces *inside* the car that filled the windows from
behind, and threw a spike under the floor. Apertures are now cut by removing faces whose centre falls inside a
traced outline, then snapping the new boundary onto that outline.

Geometry Nodes: **not used.** `geometry-script` was cloned and installed at the user's request but no
production asset depends on it. Honest gap, not a considered choice.

### 4.2 The method that works — swept sections with features as rows

This is the most transferable thing I have. `tools/front_end.py` builds the front bumper as **one cross-section
swept along a measured plan path**. Every feature of the part is a *row* of that section:

- seven rows over the 50 mm top roll-over
- five rows per rib groove
- exact rows at the number-plate recess edges
- eight rows down the tucked-under lower face
- **first and last rows are returns** — the surface turns back behind itself, so the part has no raw edge

and features fade in and out along the path with smoothstep, never step:

```python
def side_t(x):                      # 0 in the centre region, 1 at the corners
    t = min(1.0, max(0.0, (abs(x) - 0.25) / 0.40))
    return t * t * (3 - 2 * t)      # smoothstep
```

The plan path is a cubic spline through measured control points, resampled at 8 mm (`meshkit.spline_path`).
The row count is identical at every station, so the result is a clean quad grid with no triangles and no
staircase.

The front bumper and grille are the only parts of the Nova the user has consistently called good. Everything
else was a plain surface with holes cut through it afterwards — and a cut goes *across* the rows, so its edge
is a staircase of whatever cells it landed in.

### 4.3 Bringing that method to the rest of the car (2026-10-27)

Three changes, all FILE-VERIFIED and in git:

**(a) `roll_edges.py` — no raw edges anywhere.** For every boundary vertex, build a frame on the surface —
normal `n`, boundary tangent `t`, and `h = n × t` flipped to point *into the opening* — then sweep a small
section along the boundary:

```python
h = n.cross(t)
inward = sum((f.calc_center_median() - v.co) for f in v.link_faces)
if inward.dot(h) > 0:          # h points into the mesh; flip it to point into the opening
    h = -h
for d, e in profile[1:]:       # d along the surface, e behind it
    ring[v].append(bm.verts.new(v.co + h * d - n * e))
```

Five profiles: `arch` (rolls over the lip and turns back into the wheel well), `window` and `screen` (step back
into the rebate the glass sits in), `floor` (folds inboard under the car), `hem` (short fold on a free edge),
and `shut` (for an edge that butts another panel).

Two refinements that mattered: an edge that butts another panel is **detected with a BVH of the other parts**
and given a shut return that opens a real gap, instead of a flange into fresh air; and an edge the generator
has **already rolled** (the wing and sill build their own arch lips) is detected by comparing a boundary face's
normal with the one behind it, and left alone. Rolling those twice is what produced visible flaps.

**(b) Aperture edges as rows of the section.** `build_shell2.py` now makes the glass band start exactly on each
window's lower edge and end on its upper one wherever a window exists, eased in and out over 55 mm so the band
does not kink at the ends. Measured against the triangulated glass corners, aperture error went from **10–100 mm
to 13–23 mm** and the staircase disappeared from the overlays.

**(c) Shut lines as grooves with walls.** `shut_lines.py` samples a traced line onto the body with a BVH and
lays a five-row section across it — lip, wall, floor, wall, lip — so the gap catches light. Same construction
as the grille groove in `front_end.py`.

### 4.4 Glass derived from the hole, not from coordinates

`add_glass.py` does not model glass from numbers of its own. Because `roll_edges` has already turned each
aperture into a rebate, **the pane is the innermost ring of that rebate**, pushed in 4 mm and filled. It
therefore cannot disagree with its hole. Five panes: both side windows, both halves of the hatch glass, the
windscreen.

Two gotchas found and fixed: the windscreen straddles the centreline on a half-model, so it is an *open chain*
with both ends on x = 0, not a loop; and filling it as one n-gon over a non-convex outline triangulated across
itself and showed a shard through the glass — it is a fan from the centre now.

### 4.5 Procedural parts grafted onto a third-party mesh (Sunbeam / Escort)

A genuinely different problem: the host mesh is someone else's and must survive untouched. Practices that
emerged:

- **Restore-then-build.** `build_extras.restore_modified()` puts back stock meshes edited last time (kept as
  `mod_*` copies) so every build starts from the same state. This is what makes "same result every time"
  possible on a mesh the script mutates.
- **`no_export` custom property.** Objects stay in the blend but are skipped on export — cutters, collider
  hulls, cameras, the stowed stock engine. Used in all three projects.
- **Region filtering for a graft.** The Escort engine is filtered against the false bay floor, the firewall, a
  z cut and specific lettering geometry before it is accepted into the Sunbeam's bay.
- **Positional comparison is a trap.** `export_aero.compare()` is positional, so after a node is *removed*
  everything downstream reads "changed". The README records the fix: diff by node path instead.

## 6. END-TO-END ASSET AND MOD WORKFLOWS

### 6.1 Nova — from photograph to panel (the from-scratch workflow)

1. **Collect references.** Photographs of real cars (own + Wikimedia Commons, licences recorded in
   `reference/photos/commons/_licences.txt`), a 4-view line blueprint, a photographed factory GA sheet.
2. **Rectify the drawings.** `rectify_ga.py` finds the sheet's ruled frame in the photograph and warps it to a
   rectangle. Residual local distortion ≈ 2.5 %, so the standing rule became **drawings for shape, spec sheet
   for size**.
3. **Mark and solve the photographs.** Points marked at full resolution with pixel-gridded tiles
   (`marktiles.py`); bundle adjustment (`ba.py` / `ba2.py`) solves camera poses and 3D points together against
   known anchors.
4. **Trace the drawings.** `trace_side.py` etc. follow ink lines from a seed, in the car's own frame.
5. **Cross-check the two against each other** — `bp_on_photo.py` draws traced lines *and* measured points onto
   a photograph through that photograph's solved camera. **This is the step that catches errors neither source
   reveals alone.**
6. **Generate the panel** from the agreed curves, as a swept section.
7. **Check geometrically** (`geo_check.py`, signed mm from each of the 34 points) **and photographically**
   (`match_overlay.py` + `overlay_edges.py` for silhouettes, `match_look.py` for a glossy-red render through a
   solved camera — a clay render hides creases and has misled me more than once).
8. Export, deploy, drive. **Steps 8 have not been run for the current body.**

### 6.2 Sunbeam / Escort — conversion workflow

1. Import the host model losslessly (`kn5_import.py`), prove the round trip (`kn5_compare.py`).
2. Restore any previously modified stock meshes so the build starts clean.
3. Build the new parts procedurally, positioned against the host geometry.
4. Export to kn5 + LODs; prove original nodes unchanged (`export_aero.compare()`, diffed by node path).
5. Derive physics in plain Python from a Kunos donor car plus sourced real-world values; **assert** setup
   defaults match physics values and every LUT exists.
6. Stage into `staging/<car id>`; install with a script that refuses while `acs.exe` is running and archives the
   replaced version.
7. Drive; keep the previous version as a named BACKUP car and a git tag.

The separation in step 6/7 is what makes this workflow safe to iterate: there is always a drivable previous
version in the game to compare against.

## 9. TECHNIQUES THAT WORKED WELL

**Measurement discipline.** Bundle-adjusting real photographs against hard anchors (wheelbase, tracks, 520 ×
111 mm plates) gives a body of truth that survives rebuilds. Median mark error 2.5–3.3 mm on the best cameras
is good enough to argue about panel gaps with.

**Cross-checking two independent sources through a solved camera** (M015). This is the technique I would most
want another assistant to steal. Projecting a traced drawing line *and* a triangulated point onto the same
photograph, through that photograph's own camera, exposes errors that neither source reveals alone — it caught
F003 immediately and would have caught F001 months earlier.

**Swept sections with features as rows** (M006). The only part of the from-scratch car the user has praised.

**Deriving a part from a hole rather than from coordinates** (M011). Glass that is the innermost ring of its
own rebate cannot drift away from its aperture.

**Version-as-a-car** (M005). Fourteen backup cars sitting in `content/cars` means every physics change can be
A/B driven immediately. Cheap, and it has repeatedly settled "is this better?" questions.

**Builds that assert their own consistency** (M004). Asserting that every setup default equals the physics
value it adjusts, and that every referenced LUT exists, catches a class of error that is otherwise found only
by a confused driver.

**Restore-then-build on a third-party mesh** (M024). The reason the Sunbeam build is byte-reproducible.

**Installers with a process guard and an archive step** (M026). Directly prevents the class of loss in F008.

**A known-bad test fixture** (M028) — a deliberately cheated car kept in the game to prove a detector works.

**Writing the reasoning down at the time.** `docs/DECISIONS.md` is now ~600 lines of dated entries with the
evidence for each choice. Several bugs in this audit were found by re-reading an old entry and noticing its

## 10. TECHNIQUES TO REPLACE OR IMPROVE

**Cutting features into a finished grid.** Replace entirely with features as rows of the section (M010). Where
a feature does not run along the loft axis, that region needs its own parameterisation (M022) — not a cut.

**One parameterisation for a whole car body.** A station loft along `y` is right for the flanks and wrong for
the tail, and it cost me a whole phase to learn that (F011). Decide the parameterisation per region up front.

**Each panel fitting itself to a neighbour's mesh.** This is the live bug (F006). Two panels that share a shut
line must share *one curve*, each offset by half the gap. Scraping a neighbour's boundary vertices and
smoothing them is how a 4 mm gap becomes 40 mm.

**Smoothing as a fix.** The 160 mm running mean (F017 → F006b) was a reasonable response to a lumpy input, and
became a bug the moment the input stopped being lumpy. Any smoothing that compensates for a known defect should
record what it compensates for and be removed with it.

**Judging matches on downscaled images.** Replaced with full-resolution checks quoting px and mm (F021), but I
have relapsed; it needs to be a hard rule, not an intention.

**Clay renders for shape judgement.** They hide creases. Use the glossy-red matched-camera render (M020).

**Generated meshes as rulers.** Never again (M030, F023).

**Boolean on open surfaces.** Never (F009).

**Geometry Nodes: underused to the point of absence.** Every procedural operation I do is bespoke Python. Some
of it — arrays along curves, surface-conforming details, instancing trim — is what Geometry Nodes is for, and
would be inspectable by the user in a way my scripts are not.

**UV and materials: barely touched on the Nova.** Materials are colour presets with embedded DDS swatches; no
real UV work, no baked AO, no normal maps. The project's own research notes cite a published BeamNG workflow
(mask-derived normal maps for panel gaps, AO baked from a skylight and multiplied into diffuse and specular)
that I have read and not applied.

**Testing.** The Nova body has never been exported or driven. Every claim about it is geometric or
photographic. That is a real gap: EXPORT-ONLY and WORKED-IN-GAME are different things and I have been
accumulating work that has reached neither.

## 11. RECOMMENDED DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW

### Stage 1 — Research and reference validation
*Inputs:* photographs, drawings, brochure/homologation figures, licence provenance.
*Resolve first:* which exact variant; which views on a multi-view sheet are actually your car (F020); whether
each drawing is self-consistent against two independent dimensions (F019).
*Outputs:* a reference pack with recorded licences; a spec sheet with every value tagged SOURCED / ESTIMATE /
PLACEHOLDER.
*Pass/fail:* no unexplained gap in body dimensions or mass; every conflict recorded with a stated resolution.

### Stage 2 — Verified specification and frame
*Resolve first:* **one** coordinate frame, defined in **one** file that every tool imports (F001). Loaded vs
unloaded ride height stated explicitly (F014).
*Outputs:* `frame.py` or equivalent; a calibration record per drawing.
*Pass/fail:* a known landmark lands within tolerance from both the photogrammetry and the drawings — test this
before building anything.

### Stage 3 — Measured block-out
*Methods:* bundle adjustment with hard anchors (M013); leave-one-out validation (M014); cross-check through the
solved camera (M015).
*Outputs:* solved cameras + 3D points, with per-point observability.
*Pass/fail:* median mark error under ~5 mm; no point published that is seen from only one direction (F003);
the solve's input hash matches the result file (F002).

### Stage 4 — Geometry
*Methods:* swept sections with every feature as a row and returns at both ends (M006); a region that the main
parameterisation cannot express gets its own (M022); adjacent panels share one shut curve.
*Outputs:* one script per panel, deterministic, re-runnable.
*Pass/fail:* no raw boundary anywhere; panel gaps within ±1 mm of target; no face normal flipped; grid rows
land on every named feature.

### Stage 5 — UV, materials, paint
*Methods:* UV seams on hard edges; panel-gap normal maps derived from masks rather than from geometry; AO baked
from a skylight.
*Pass/fail:* no stretching on long thin trim; texel density consistent; glass blending set correctly.

### Stage 6 — Hierarchy, pivots, animation
*Outputs:* helper empties at measured positions (`WHEEL_xx`, `SUSP_xx`, `COCKPIT_HR`, `STEER_HR`); `DISK_xx`
matching `brakes.ini`; KSANIM per opening panel.
*Pass/fail:* every node name an ini references exists; pivots on the real hinge axis.

### Stage 7 — Export
*Methods:* own KN5 writer (M001) with a round-trip check (M002); `no_export` for working geometry (M025).
*Pass/fail:* re-import and diff **by node path** (F025); LOD chain present; vertex counts under the split
threshold.

### Stage 8 — Game data and physics
*Methods:* derive from a named donor, assert setup defaults match physics values and LUTs exist (M004); fit
colliders and ride height from the model and the running game (M027).
*Pass/fail:* every assertion passes; ride height measured in game matches the design value.

### Stage 9 — In-game test
*Pass/fail:* car loads; wheels in the arches and on the ground; no z-fighting; LODs switch cleanly; drives;
telemetry sane.

### Stage 10 — Regression and release
*Methods:* previous version kept as a named BACKUP car and a git tag (M005, F030); installers archive first and
refuse while the game runs (M026).

## 14. UNRESOLVED TECHNICAL QUESTIONS

1. **How should two adjacent panels share a shut line so the gap is constant by construction?** My current
   answer — one curve, each panel offset by half the gap — is untested. There may be a standard way that also
   handles the corner where three panels meet.
2. **Is a station loft the right structure for a car body at all?** It cannot represent a vertical rear face
   (F011). Patch surfaces per region, a subdivision cage, or a proper CAD surface are all alternatives I have
   not evaluated — except FreeCAD, which failed on performance (F022).
3. **Is there a better way to get an aperture edge onto the grid than forcing section rows onto it?** Forcing
   rows works where the feature runs along the loft axis and not otherwise.
4. **What is the right topology target for an AC body?** I have no evidence-based view on polycount, loop
   placement or where to split smoothing groups. My research notes cite a published description (one smoothing
   group per shell, split only at panel cutlines) which I have not tested.
5. **How should LODs be generated?** The Sunbeam builds LOD b/c/d by script; I have never measured whether they
   switch cleanly or what the real budget is.
6. **How far can physics honestly be derived from a donor?** v19 → v20 → v26 is two full re-bases of the same
   car. I do not have a principled method for choosing a donor.
7. **What in-game validation is actually sufficient?** "It drives" is the current bar. Telemetry-based
   acceptance tests exist (Setup Coach) but are not wired into the build.

## 17. ONE-PAGE EXCHANGE SUMMARY

   everywhere.

   one curve. *Still open.*
4. **F011 — a station loft cannot hold a vertical rear face.** Choose the parameterisation per region.
5. **F023/F030 — a generated mesh is not a measurement, and a backup must never look like the latest version.**
