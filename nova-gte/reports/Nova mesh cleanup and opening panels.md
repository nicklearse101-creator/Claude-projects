# Borrow clean normals, then hinge the panels

The quickest way to make the Nova's ~1.2M-triangle body shade smoothly without changing its shape is to leave the vertices where they are and **copy custom normals onto the body from a heavily smoothed duplicate** with Blender's Data Transfer modifier. The shape does not move at all. Creases and panel-gap lips are protected with sharp edges and vertex groups. Headless in Blender 5.2 on a 1.3M-triangle test mesh, the whole step took seconds. Real geometry edits come second and stay local: masked smoothing on the rippled bumper and sill bands, relax tools on jagged lines, and small quad patches shrinkwrapped over the genuinely crumpled panels. Full retopology is how professionals build car bodies, but here it would amount to remodelling the car. The traps are about data, not speed. A proxy that still carries the old noisy custom normals transfers the noise straight back. "Free" float custom normals make the transfer silently do nothing. Re-triangulating after the normals are set bent them by up to 55° in testing. The Laplacian Smooth modifier costs about 100 s per iteration at this size. On the Assetto Corsa side, a kn5 vertex holds exactly one normal and a tangent with no handedness, so AC shades with exactly the corner normals the exporter writes, and the project's exporter already reads the evaluated mesh without re-triangulating it. For opening panels, each door, the bonnet and the tailgate become meshes parented to an empty whose origin sits on the hinge axis. A `.ksanim` file rotates that empty. The format is a trivially simple binary list of per-frame local transforms, and the Escort project already writes it from Python. Vanilla AC recognises only `car_door_L/R.ksanim` and gives the player no in-game control for them. A bonnet, a tailgate and doors the player can open all need CSP's `[ANIMATION_...]` sections bound to extra switches, and Nik already has those switches on Alt/Ctrl+Alt + numpad keys.

## Borrowed normals hide the ripples without moving a vertex

The earlier bilateral denoise, capped at 1 mm, barely helped for a structural reason. The reflection streaks on the bumpers and sills come from surface undulation several millimetres to centimetres long, and a filter that may move a vertex only 1 mm cannot flatten a wave that long. A normal-only method sidesteps the cap: the *source* of the normals can be smoothed as hard as needed, because only its shading is borrowed and the exported shape stays identical. This is standard game-car practice. Polycount advice for racing-game cars is to build a smooth high-poly and "transfer vertex normals" to the low-poly, "like baking a normal map to the vertices", which avoids texture compression and interpolation artifacts. A second user reported it "works reasonably well, but its not perfect" and works better from a source at subdivision level 1 or 2 (forum excerpts only) ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2426371); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2787669)). Polycount commenters also said hand-averaging normals is "(usually) not suitable" here, and that a commercial normal-thief add-on only shortcuts what Blender's Data Transfer already does ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2425875)). A Blender Artists tutorial series describes the same idea as modelling the shapes you want separately and then stealing "their Normals" for the main mesh ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/customizing-normals-tutorial-series/1369038)).

Blender's tool for this is the **Data Transfer modifier with Face Corner Data → Custom Normals**. Its **Topology** mapping "simply matches the elements based on their index" and is "best suited for a destination mesh that's a deformed copy of the source". That is exactly what a smoothed twin of the body is: same vertex count and order, different positions. For a remeshed or retopologised proxy, **Nearest Face Interpolated** or **Projected Face Interpolated** apply instead. **Max Distance** rejects far matches, so a corner on a door shut face cannot pick up the outer skin's normal, and a **Vertex Group** gives per-element control of the Mix Factor ([Blender Manual: Data Transfer](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/modify/data_transfer.html)). Since Blender 4.1 there is no Auto Smooth to switch on first. Meshes behave as if Auto Smooth were on at 180°, and custom normals are used whenever they exist ([Blender 4.1 Modeling notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.1/modeling/)). Older forum advice that the transfer "only store[s] custom normal data for Auto Smooth mode" is therefore obsolete.

Build the proxy with the fast smoothers. In a headless Blender 5.2.1 test on a 1,310,720-triangle noisy ellipsoid (positional noise 0.4 mm, mean normal error 2.77°), **Corrective Smooth in Only Smooth mode, Length Weighted, 20 iterations, applied in 0.39 s** and cut the proxy's normal error to 0.50°. Ten passes of `bmesh.ops.smooth_vert` took 1.73 s and reached 0.32°. The **Laplacian Smooth modifier needed 198.8 s for just two iterations** ([local test results](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/research_notes/Nova%20mesh%20cleanup%20and%20opening%20panels/bench_normals_blender52_results.txt)). Corrective Smooth's Only Smooth mode applies "smoothing without reconstructing the surface", and Pin Boundaries "excludes boundary vertices from being smoothed" ([bpy CorrectiveSmoothModifier](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.CorrectiveSmoothModifier.html)). Creases are protected three ways. True creases and gap lips are left out of the smoothing vertex group, so the proxy keeps them sharp. They are marked sharp on the body (the `sharp_edge` attribute), so shading still breaks there. And the transfer's own vertex group fades the effect near them. One step is easy to miss: **the proxy must lose its inherited `custom_normal` attribute before the transfer**. The transfer reads the source's evaluated corner normals, custom normals included, so a duplicate that keeps the body's noisy normals simply hands the noise back ([data_transfer.cc](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/data_transfer.cc)). The local test stripped it from every proxy for that reason.

```python
import bpy
body = bpy.data.objects["body"]                       # painted shell, FINAL topology and sharp edges
cn = body.data.attributes.get("custom_normal")
assert cn is None or cn.data_type == 'INT16_2D', cn.data_type   # FLOAT_VECTOR -> transfer is a silent no-op
body.data.shade_smooth()                              # clears sharp_face; sharp_edge marks on creases/gaps stay

# 1) smoothed twin: same topology, its own clean normals
proxy = body.copy(); proxy.data = body.data.copy(); proxy.name = "body_NormalSource"
bpy.context.scene.collection.objects.link(proxy)
if "custom_normal" in proxy.data.attributes:
    proxy.data.attributes.remove(proxy.data.attributes["custom_normal"])
cs = proxy.modifiers.new("cs", 'CORRECTIVE_SMOOTH')
cs.use_only_smooth = True; cs.smooth_type = 'LENGTH_WEIGHTED'
cs.factor = 0.5; cs.iterations = 20; cs.use_pin_boundary = True
cs.vertex_group = "smooth_ok"                         # crease rows and gap lips NOT in this group

# 2) borrow its normals
dt = body.modifiers.new("NormalsFromTwin", 'DATA_TRANSFER')
dt.object = proxy
dt.use_loop_data = True; dt.data_types_loops = {'CUSTOM_NORMAL'}
dt.loop_mapping = 'TOPOLOGY'                          # index match; 'POLYINTERP_NEAREST' for a remeshed proxy
dt.mix_mode = 'REPLACE'; dt.mix_factor = 1.0          # set explicitly (API doc and source disagree on default)
dt.vertex_group = "transfer_ok"                       # optional fade away from creases/gaps
with bpy.context.temp_override(object=body, active_object=body,
                               selected_objects=[body], selected_editable_objects=[body]):
    bpy.ops.object.modifier_apply(modifier=dt.name, merge_customdata=False)
```

Create both vertex groups on the body before copying it, because the twin inherits them. In the benchmark, every mapping (NEAREST_NORMAL, NEAREST_POLYNOR, POLYINTERP_NEAREST, POLYINTERP_LNORPROJ) brought the destination to the **same ~0.50° residual** from a smooth proxy. They differed only in cost, from 1.54 s to 5.73 s per evaluation. Applying through `modifier_apply` under `temp_override` worked headless in 2.63 s ([local test results](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/research_notes/Nova%20mesh%20cleanup%20and%20opening%20panels/bench_normals_blender52_results.txt)). Topology mapping was not timed, but it is an index lookup with no ray casts, so it should be the cheapest. Apply the modifier rather than leaving it live. Otherwise every depsgraph update and every export re-runs seconds of transfer on a 1.2M-triangle mesh. Three limits stay. The method changes shading only, so the silhouette and the actual zig-zag path of a triangulated crease remain: the highlight will still be cut by a jagged sharp edge. A strongly smoothed source rounds any crease it is allowed to touch. And nobody has published this exact recipe on a car body. It follows directly from the manual's definition of Topology mapping, but it is untested on the Nova. Trial it on the front bumper first, and judge it the way game car artists do, with a reflective matcap from several angles ([The Rookies](https://www.therookies.co/blog/breakdowns/production-process-vehicle-assets-games)).

## Real reshaping is reserved for silhouettes, jagged lines and crumpled panels

Normals cannot fix what shows in the outline or in grazing reflections, and they cannot straighten a ragged line. For those, the useful split is between cheap masked tools and expensive rebuilds. The table ranks the options for this body by value. The rankings combine the Blender manual's own descriptions, practitioner reports and the local benchmark.

| Method | Shape change | Cost on this body | Fixes | Leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal transfer from smoothed twin | None | Seconds, scriptable | Streaks, ripples in shading | Silhouette, crease path |
| Masked vertex smoothing (Corrective / bmesh / Laplacian on a region) | Small, measurable | Seconds (Laplacian: minutes) | Real ripples on bumpers/sills | Needs a re-transfer afterwards |
| Sculpt Relax / Relax Face Sets / Surface Smooth | Small, local | Interactive | Jagged crease and gap lines | Untested on this mesh |
| Local quad patch + Subdivision 1-2 + Shrinkwrap | Local rebuild | Hours per panel | Crumpled panels, worst creases | Stitching into the triangle mesh |
| Auto quad remesh (QuadriFlow, Quad Remesher) | Large | Minutes + heavy cleanup | Broad curvature | Breaks small bevels and panel lines |
| Full manual retopology | Rebuild | Days | Everything | Effectively a remodel |

The **Smooth Laplacian** modifier is built for exactly this kind of mesh. The manual says it is meant to "reduce noise on a mesh's surface with minimal changes to its shape" on objects "reconstructed from the real world". It has Preserve Volume, a separate Lambda Border for open edges, and vertex-group masking. The same manual warns that meshes over 10,000 vertices "may take several minutes" ([Blender Manual: Smooth Laplacian](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/laplacian_smooth.html)). The 99 s-per-iteration benchmark confirms the warning, so run it only on a separated bumper or sill region. For the whole body, use Corrective Smooth or `bmesh.ops.smooth_vert` restricted to the ripple bands, with every axis flag set True: the bmesh flags default to False, and then nothing moves ([bmesh.ops](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bmesh.ops.html)). For the jagged lines, the most direct built-in tool is the sculpt Mesh Filter **Relax Face Sets**, which "will remove the jagged lines visible after drawing or creating a face set". Give each panel, or each side of a crease, its own face set, and the filter evens out the boundary between them. **Surface Smooth** "eliminates irregularities ... while preserving the volume", **Relax** redistributes vertices "without deforming the volume", and every filter respects masks ([Blender Manual: Mesh Filter](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/sculpt_paint/sculpting/tools/mesh_filter.html)). Two rules go with any of these tools. First, measure the displacement against an untouched copy to stay inside the 1-2 mm budget. Second, re-run the normal transfer afterwards, because the geometry has changed.

For the genuinely crumpled small panels, the professional answer is a clean local patch: a coarse quad grid drawn over the area (Retopology overlay, Poly Build, face snapping), Subdivision Surface at level 1-2, then **Shrinkwrap with Target Normal Project**, which gives "a much smoother projection in return for being significantly slower" ([Blender Manual: Shrinkwrap](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/shrinkwrap.html)). In the local test, wrapping a smoothed 1.3M-triangle proxy back onto the noisy body took only 0.21 s, because the two surfaces nearly coincided ([local test results](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/research_notes/Nova%20mesh%20cleanup%20and%20opening%20panels/bench_normals_blender52_results.txt)). There is one trap. A dense cage wrapped onto a noisy target copies the noise into the patch. Keep the cage coarse, as Polycount's level-1-2 advice implies, and wrap it onto the smoothed twin rather than the raw body. The Erase Displacement mesh filter exists specifically to clean "reprojection artifacts after applying a Shrinkwrap" ([Blender Manual: Mesh Filter](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/sculpt_paint/sculpting/tools/mesh_filter.html)).

Automatic quad remeshing is poor value as final geometry. A Blender Artists user found that with Quad Remesher on small rounded bevels "the curvature always ends up breaking", although a car wheel arch came out "practically spot on", and that "Lots of cleanup is needed afterwards" ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/quad-remesher-auto-retopologizer/1170913?page=30)). Blender's Voxel remesh "should not be used for ... Generating a mesh for applying the Subdivision Surface Modifier" ([Blender Manual: Remeshing](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/retopology.html)). Remeshers are useful as a *normals source*, not as the exported body. Full retopology is what professionals start from: keep the mesh "in quads to ensure no pinching" ([The Rookies](https://www.therookies.co/blog/breakdowns/production-process-vehicle-assets-games)), and "plan topology in advance, before moving to surfaces creation" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/vehicle-production-for-games/)). On a finished shape it is a remodel that can drift from the photos.

Two often-suggested tools fit this body badly. **Weighted Normal** keeps big flat faces dominant over small bevel faces ([Polycount Wiki](http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/Face_weighted_normals); [Blender Manual: Weighted Normal](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/normals/weighted_normal.html)). On a dense, evenly sized, curved mesh it changes little and cannot remove noise. Its one plausible use, untested, is calming sliver triangles beside a triangulated crease, with Face Area mode on a crease-band group. **Baking a normal map** needs a clean low-poly first, would bake today's ripples into the map, and is not how car-game artists shade bodies. BeamNG modder Gabester "almost never" bakes normal maps for whole cars, and the Rookies Ford GT baked only its wheels ([BeamNG](https://beamng.com/posts/37582/); [The Rookies](https://www.therookies.co/blog/breakdowns/production-process-vehicle-assets-games)). The convention worth copying is Gabester's: the whole shell in "one smoothing group, except at panel cutlines", panels spaced "a few millimeters" apart, and AO baked to darken the gaps ([BeamNG](https://beamng.com/posts/37582/)). Older cars ran gaps up to about a quarter inch (~6 mm) against ~3.5 mm for today's best ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/cars/learn/finding-the-right-car/why-car-panel-gaps-matter/3432)), so keep the Nova's existing gap widths rather than tightening them.

## Blender 5.x and the kn5 format punish data mistakes, not slow scripts

The Blender 4.1 normals rewrite changes every script written from older tutorials. `Mesh.use_auto_smooth` and `auto_smooth_angle` are gone. Smoothing by angle is now a modifier or operator acting on the `sharp_edge` attribute. `calc_normals_split` gave way to the always-current `Mesh.corner_normals`. `MeshLoop.normal` is read-only, and custom normals are set with `normals_split_custom_set` (per corner) or `normals_split_custom_set_from_vertices` ([Blender 4.1 Python API notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.1/python_api/)). In 5.2, `Mesh.set_sharp_from_angle()` fills `sharp_edge`, `shade_smooth()` clears `sharp_face`, and `has_custom_normals` and `normals_domain` report the state ([bpy.types.Mesh](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.Mesh.html)). `bpy.ops.object.shade_auto_smooth` worked headless under `temp_override` and added a "Smooth by Angle" geometry-nodes modifier ([local test results](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/research_notes/Nova%20mesh%20cleanup%20and%20opening%20panels/bench_normals_blender52_results.txt)).

The storage format explains most of the pitfalls. Data Transfer and Weighted Normal write `custom_normal` as **INT16_2D on the face-corner domain**, the old "corner fan space" encoding, which stores each normal *relative to the automatic normal of its smoothing fan* ([mesh_normals.cc](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/mesh_normals.cc)). Anything that changes the fans after the normals are set therefore changes the final normals. That includes re-triangulating, merging by distance, decimating, adding a Smooth by Angle modifier later in the stack, calling `set_sharp_from_angle`, or separating a panel. Blender 4.5 added a second, "free" storage type: FLOAT_VECTOR directions that are "not updated for vertex deformations" ([commit d3f84449ad](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/commit/d3f84449ad); [4.5 Modeling notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.5/modeling/)). The modifiers do not convert between the two. On a mesh carrying free normals, the local test found that **Weighted Normal and `normals_split_custom_set_from_vertices` changed nothing and raised no error**. The Data Transfer source likewise returns unchanged when it cannot get a short2 corner span ([MOD_weighted_normal.cc](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/source/blender/modifiers/intern/MOD_weighted_normal.cc); [data_transfer.cc](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/data_transfer.cc)). That is what the assert in the recipe above guards against.

Triangulation is the sharpest edge. On a 160,000-quad test grid with custom normals set, the maximum deviation from the intended normals was 0.26°. After `bmesh.ops.triangulate`, or a Triangulate modifier with `keep_custom_normals=False`, it was **55.3°**. With `keep_custom_normals=True`, or when only `calc_loop_triangles()` was read, it stayed at 0.26° ([local test results](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/research_notes/Nova%20mesh%20cleanup%20and%20opening%20panels/bench_normals_blender52_results.txt)). The Nova exporter takes the safe path: it reads `loop_triangles` from the evaluated mesh and never rewrites topology ([ac_kn5_export.py](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/tools/ac_kn5_export.py)). The one `bmesh.ops.triangulate` in the project builds the GTE lettering on fresh geometry before any custom normals exist, so it is harmless as written ([details_ref.py](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/blender/scripts/details_ref.py)). A few smaller traps apply too. The API reference lists the Data Transfer `mix_factor` default as 0.0, but the modifier's `init_data` sets 1.0, so always set it explicitly ([bpy DataTransferModifier](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.DataTransferModifier.html); [MOD_datatransfer.cc](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/source/blender/modifiers/intern/MOD_datatransfer.cc)). Weighted Normal tests vertex-group *membership*, not weight, so a vertex painted at 0 still counts ([MOD_weighted_normal.cc](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/source/blender/modifiers/intern/MOD_weighted_normal.cc)). Keep Data Transfer last in the stack. Bug titles report broken normals with a deform modifier after it and corrupted normals after triangulation ([#94838](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/94838); [T44878](https://developer.blender.org/T44878)). A 4.1-alpha report about partial vertex-group weights in Data Transfer has no known fix status ([#117520](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/117520)), and the benchmark used only full weights, so test a soft fade on 5.x before trusting it.

On the AC side, the kn5 vertex is just **position, normal, UV and tangent (float3), with 16-bit indices**. There are no smoothing groups and no bitangent sign ([Kn5Node.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools/Kn5File/Kn5Node.cs); [Kn5Writer.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools/Kn5File/Kn5Writer.cs)). A "hard edge" in AC is simply two vertices with different normals. Every place where Blender's corner normals differ, whether from `sharp_edge`, `sharp_face` or a discontinuity in the custom normals, becomes a visible split, which is right at shut lines and wrong on continuous paint. The Nova exporter writes `corner_normals` directly. It splits meshes at 65,535 vertices, but those splits create no shading seams, because the vertices duplicated at a split keep the same normal. It computes MikkTSpace tangents but falls back to an arbitrary perpendicular tangent if the mesh has n-gons, and it never reads `bitangent_sign`. One branch matters for this work: for any object flagged `kn5_pristine` that carries `kn5_nrm`/`kn5_tan` attributes, the exporter **writes those stored normals instead of the corner normals**, so a transfer onto such an object would be silently ignored ([ac_kn5_export.py, lines 124-189](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/tools/ac_kn5_export.py)). Only the shared `kn5_import.py` used in the Escort and Sunbeam projects sets that flag, and nothing in the Nova scripts does today. Still, clear it on any part that ever came in through that importer. Because the exporter writes final normals directly, the FBX question of whether ksEditor keeps custom normals does not arise. Kunos' pipeline does say FBX "custom normals are supported" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf); [Scribd mirror](https://www.scribd.com/document/501206878/AC-Pipeline-PUB-Rev2-0)). The same PDF budgets **125,000 triangles for the entire LOD A exterior** and asks LODs to keep "the curved parts smooth" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). The Nova body alone is about ten times that budget. Do not count on CSP to smooth normals at load time either: `[SMOOTH_NORMALS_...]` is documented only for tracks, and because it averages by position it would also round the gap lips ([CSP docs: Tracks/Meshes manipulation](https://cup.acstuff.club/docs/csp/tracks/meshes-manipulation)). No source says which bitangent handedness AC's shaders assume. That matters only if the paint, or a part with mirrored UVs, uses a normal map.

## A .ksanim is named local transforms, and Python can write it

The .ksanim format is a small little-endian binary file. It starts with an int32 version and an int32 node count. Each node then has an int32-length-prefixed name and an int32 frame count, followed by one full **local transform per frame, relative to the node's kn5 parent** ([KsAnimReader.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools/KsAnimFile/KsAnimReader.cs); [KsAnim.FromFile.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools/KsAnimFile/KsAnim.FromFile.cs)). Version 1 stores a 16-float matrix per frame. Version 2 stores a quaternion (x, y, z, w), a translation and a scale, which is 40 bytes per frame. Kunos ships both: 1,034 v2 files and 8 v1 files across the stock cars on this PC, and **113 of the 115 stock door clips have exactly 100 frames per node** (measured over [content/cars/ks_*/animations](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/)). There is no fps or duration field; the playback time comes from the config (`TIME`) or an engine default. Content Manager's reader and writer confirm the layout ([KsAnimWriter.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools/KsAnimFile/KsAnimWriter.cs)). So does a byte count on the Miata door clip: 4 nodes × 100 frames is exactly the 16,074-byte file ([ks_mazda_miata car_door_L.ksanim](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/ks_mazda_miata/animations/car_door_L.ksanim)). Frames are absolute, not deltas. The Miata's `DOOR_L` frame 0 equals that dummy's local matrix in `mazda_miata.kn5`, translation (0.789, 0.527, 0.536) and identity rotation ([mazda_miata.kn5](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/ks_mazda_miata/mazda_miata.kn5)).

| Offset | Field | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | version (2) | int32 |
| 4 | node count | int32 |
| per node | name length, name bytes (ASCII) | int32 + bytes |
| per node | frame count | int32 |
| per frame (v2) | qx, qy, qz, qw, tx, ty, tz, sx, sy, sz | 10 × float32 |

Kunos' own route animates nulls in 3ds Max, exports an FBX holding only the animated nulls, and loads it in ksEditor with **File > Open FBX Animation**, which "automatically saves a clip_name.ksanim file in the same folder" as the FBX ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). Blender users who tried that route, or the old "ksanim/knh exporter 0.4" written for Blender 2.80, hit predictable bugs. The rest pose came out "at the end of the animation rather than the start", the animated mesh would "vanish entirely", and a wiper ended up "flipped back 90 degrees" ([assettocorsamods: FBX Animation](https://assettocorsamods.net/threads/fbx-animation.2498/); [overtake.gg wiper thread, excerpt](https://overtake.gg/threads/help-needed-wiper-animation-odd-texture-and-missing-bits.228877)). All three are symptoms of clip frames that do not use the same axis conversion and parent chain as the kn5 nodes. The Nova pipeline avoids them by construction. Its exporter writes kn5 nodes directly with a `TO_AC` matrix, and the Escort project already contains `write_door_ksanim()`, which builds a v2 clip from the door empty's kn5 rest pose with the same `TO_AC` and an ease-in/out ([model_fixes.py](file:///D:/Claude/escort_mk2_dtm/tools/blender/model_fixes.py); [ac_kn5_export.py](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/tools/ac_kn5_export.py)). Generalising it from a fixed Y axis to any axis and several nodes covers the bonnet, the tailgate and Kunos-style door pairs:

```python
import math, struct
from mathutils import Quaternion

def write_panel_ksanim(path, tracks, frames=100):
    """tracks: [(kn5_name, rest, axis, open_deg)]; rest = the node's local 4x4 in AC axes,
    exactly what the kn5 stores (TO_AC @ m @ TO_AC_INV); axis in AC axes (+X left, +Y up, +Z fwd)."""
    buf = struct.pack("<ii", 2, len(tracks))
    for name, rest, axis, deg in tracks:
        nm = name.encode("ascii")
        buf += struct.pack("<i", len(nm)) + nm + struct.pack("<i", frames)
        t, q0 = rest.to_translation(), rest.to_quaternion()
        for k in range(frames):
            u = k / (frames - 1); u = u * u * (3 - 2 * u)      # ease; frame 0 == rest pose exactly
            q = q0 @ Quaternion(axis, math.radians(deg) * u)
            buf += struct.pack("<10f", q.x, q.y, q.z, q.w, t.x, t.y, t.z, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0)
    with open(path, "wb") as f:
        f.write(buf)
```

Three rules come from how the clips are played. First, **frame 0 must be bit-identical to the rest matrix in the kn5**, or the part jumps when the clip takes control. Add an exporter assert for it. Second, **use around 100 frames even for a simple swing**. Kunos says the "'close' and 'open' frames are enough" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)), but Content Manager blends neighbouring frame *matrices* linearly rather than slerping quaternions ([KsAnimAnimator.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools.Render/Kn5Specific/Animations/KsAnimAnimator.cs); [KsAnimExtension.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools.Render/Kn5Specific/Animations/KsAnimExtension.cs)), which shrinks a part mid-swing between distant keys. How acs.exe itself interpolates is undocumented. Third, **list only nodes you move**, because every listed node's local matrix is overwritten each tick. Kunos animates only the opening: closing is the same clip played in reverse ("Do not animate the closing sequence!"), and "Never animate the mesh. Always animate the NULLs only!" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). Opening angles and directions were decoded from real clips. AC's axes are +X = car's left, +Y up, +Z forward. Decoded quaternions on the Lotus Carlton mod are y = −0.4226 for the left door (50°), x = −0.3837 for the bonnet (45.1°) and x = +0.5736 for the boot (70°) ([bg_lotus_carlton animations](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/bg_lotus_carlton/animations/)). The Miata door swings 65° and the AE86 door about 75° ([ks_toyota_ae86 animations](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/ks_toyota_ae86/animations/)). The Escort writer uses 70°.

| Nova panel | Hinge (empty origin) | Axis (AC) | Sign | Reference angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Left door `DOOR_L` | Front hinge line, left | Y | negative | 50-75° (Lotus 50, Miata 65, AE86 ~75) |
| Right door `DOOR_R` | Front hinge line, right | Y | positive | same |
| Bonnet, scuttle-hinged | Rear edge at the scuttle | X | negative (flip if front-hinged) | Lotus 45° |
| Tailgate | Roof rear edge | X | positive | Lotus boot 70° |

The signs follow the right-hand rule in AC's frame: a negative turn about Y swings a left door's rear edge outward. Check them in the showroom before trusting them. The Lotus boot clip also moves its translation, from (1.0698, −1.769) to (1.1351, −1.7002) in y/z. That shows an offset hinge (a gooseneck, or a tailgate pivoting behind its skin) can be faked by animating translation alongside rotation. The AE86's nodes carry a −90° X rest rotation from 3ds Max, so their swing axis reads as local Z. Keeping the Blender empties at **identity rest rotation**, as the Lotus Carlton does, keeps every clip a pure turn about one parent axis.

## Vanilla AC knows two door clips; CSP extra switches open everything

Strings inside `acs.exe` show the vanilla game loading `animations/car_door_L.ksanim` and `car_door_R.ksanim`, `lights.ksanim`, the shift and steer clips, `data/wing_animations.ini` and `data/extra_animations.ini`, and nothing for a bonnet or boot ([acs.exe](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/acs.exe)). Kunos documents the recognised names as "managed by the game engine", with door clips playing ping-pong ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). However, **no source found says when vanilla AC actually plays the door clip on track or in the pits**, and the research found no vanilla key for it. The only vanilla way to make a bonnet or tailgate player-operable is a hack. Add an aero-neutral `[WING_n]` that the player can adjust in the setup menu (a `setup.ini` entry such as `MIN=0 MAX=1 STEP=1`), then tie the clip to it in `wing_animations.ini` with `WING=n FILE=bonnet.ksanim MIN=0 MAX=1`. Content Manager's own switch generator works on the same principle ([KsAnimSwitchGenerator.xaml.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcManager/Pages/ContentTools/KsAnimSwitchGenerator.xaml.cs)), but the hack is untested in game or online. Several mods on this PC carry such wing entries for hoods with no matching wing in `aero.ini`, and actually drive them through CSP. `ford_escort_mk2_tarmac` is one: `WING=6 FILE=hood_anim.ksanim` with no `WING_6`, and `[ANIMATION_1] ... BIND_TO_EXTRA_D=1` in its ext_config ([content/cars](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars)). Content Manager's Custom Showroom is a separate, CSP-independent player. It opens left and right doors from those fixed file names over 1 s with the door sound, toggles each `wing_animations.ini` entry and each `extra_animations.ini [ANIMATION_n]` extra, and **hot-reloads a .ksanim when the file changes**, which makes it the fastest test bench ([CarData.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools.Render/Data/CarData.cs); [Kn5RenderableCar.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools.Render/Kn5Specific/Objects/Kn5RenderableCar.cs); [KsAnimAnimator.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools.Render/Kn5Specific/Animations/KsAnimAnimator.cs)). Its preview settings also carry `LeftDoorOpen`/`RightDoorOpen`, so generated previews can show doors open ([CmPreviewsSettings.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcManager/CustomShowroom/CmPreviewsSettings.cs)).

| Capability | Vanilla AC | With CSP (0.2.12-preview1 installed) |
|---|---|---|
| Door clips `car_door_L/R.ksanim` | Loaded; in-game trigger not found | Bound to any extra switch |
| Bonnet / tailgate clips | No slot; setup-menu wing hack only | `[ANIMATION_...]` + extra switch |
| Player key | None | `EXTRA_A`…`EXTRA_F`, bound in CM Controls, Patch section |
| Lock while moving | — | `SWITCH_x_FLAGS = STATIONARY_ONLY` |
| Showroom toggles | CM Custom Showroom (doors, wings, extras) | Same |
| Bonnet damage | `damage.ini` wobble on `MOTORHOOD` | Plus `[DEFORMING_HOOD]` |
| Door mirrors follow door | Not documented | Real Mirrors since 0.2.1 |
| Driver gets in/out | No clip exists | No clip exists |

CSP has **no dedicated door, hood or trunk section**. Opening panels use the generic `[ANIMATION_...]` block. `INPUT` (or `BIND_TO`) names the driver, `FILE` the clip in `animations/` and `TIME` the duration. Options are `INPUT_THRESHOLD`/`INPUT_THRESHOLD_INVERSE`, `INPUT_AS_PROGRESS` (the input sets the position directly, and loops are ignored), `LOOP_WHILE_ACTIVE`, `LOOP_KEEP_UNTIL_DONE`, `TICK_TOCK_MODE`, `AFFECTS_INTERIOR_SHAPE` (updates the interior reflection mask while playing, at some cost), `HOLD_STATE` (commented only "for hoods and such"), `BLEND_STATE` and `LUT`. In the default trigger mode the clip runs to 100% while the input is over its threshold and back to 0% otherwise ([CSP docs: Cars/Animations](https://cup.acstuff.club/docs/csp/cars/animations)). Cars get six extra switches, `EXTRA_A`…`EXTRA_F`, since CSP 0.1.76. `[EXTRA_SWITCHES]` gives each switch a role label (`SWITCH_C = ...`) and flags (`HOLD_MODE`, `STATIONARY_ONLY`, `NEURAL_GEAR_ONLY` [sic], `REQUIRES_BRAKE`). Players bind the switches in the Patch section of Content Manager's controls settings, and they appear in `controls.ini` as `__EXT_LIGHT_A`…`F` ([CSP docs: Cars/Extra switches](https://cup.acstuff.club/docs/csp/cars/extra-switches); [overtake.gg Porsche 911 GT3 CSP, user comment](https://overtake.gg/downloads/porsche-911-gt3-2022-csp-features.43619/updates)). On Nik's PC they are already bound: **A = Alt+Num7, B = Alt+Num8, C = Alt+Num9, D = Alt+Num+, E = Ctrl+Alt+Num7, F = Ctrl+Alt+Num8** ([controls.ini](file:///C:/Users/nickl/Documents/Assetto%20Corsa/cfg/controls.ini)). Many released mods use an undocumented `BIND_TO_EXTRA_C=1` form instead of `INPUT = EXTRA_C`; a scan of the 857 cars on this PC found 95 uses of `BIND_TO_EXTRA_C` alone ([content/cars/*/extension/ext_config.ini](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/)). The Lotus Carlton, the closest relative on disk, gives every panel its own switch that way ([bg_lotus_carlton ext_config](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/bg_lotus_carlton/extension/ext_config.ini)). The GT4 Alpine A110 shows the label-plus-`STATIONARY_ONLY` pattern ([gt4_alpine_a110 ext_config](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/gt4_alpine_a110/extension/ext_config.ini)). The Nova's ext_config holds only indicator and reverse emissives today, so all six switches are free. A block on the documented syntax, keeping the Escort habit of C for doors and D for the bonnet ([Escort ext_config](file:///D:/Claude/escort_mk2_dtm/archive/replaced_20261006_035100/ext_config.ini)), would be:

```ini
[EXTRA_SWITCHES]
SWITCH_C = Driver door
SWITCH_C_FLAGS = STATIONARY_ONLY
SWITCH_D = Bonnet
SWITCH_D_FLAGS = STATIONARY_ONLY
SWITCH_E = Tailgate
SWITCH_E_FLAGS = STATIONARY_ONLY
SWITCH_F = Passenger door
SWITCH_F_FLAGS = STATIONARY_ONLY

[ANIMATION_...]
INPUT = EXTRA_C
FILE = car_door_R.ksanim      ; RHD: driver door is the right door (AC +X = car's left)
TIME = 1.5
AFFECTS_INTERIOR_SHAPE = 1

[ANIMATION_...]
INPUT = EXTRA_F
FILE = car_door_L.ksanim
TIME = 1.5
AFFECTS_INTERIOR_SHAPE = 1

[ANIMATION_...]
INPUT = EXTRA_D
FILE = bonnet.ksanim
TIME = 2.0

[ANIMATION_...]
INPUT = EXTRA_E
FILE = tailgate.ksanim
TIME = 2.0
AFFECTS_INTERIOR_SHAPE = 1
```

On Nik's keys this gives Alt+Num9 for the driver door, Alt+Num+ for the bonnet, Ctrl+Alt+Num7 for the tailgate and Ctrl+Alt+Num8 for the passenger door. It is a design built from the documented keys and has not been tested. Real `TIME` values run from 1 s (Escort) to 1.5 s (Alpine) and 3 s (Lotus). Keep the standard door clip names, because vanilla, CM's showroom and CSP all look for them. `STATIONARY_ONLY` stops the switch changing while moving; whether an already-open panel stays open once the car moves is undocumented. Around this core, CSP adds a few door hooks. Lua's `car.isDriverDoorOpen` and `ac.setDriverDoorOpen(carIndex, isOpen, instant)` toggle the driver-door animation ([lua-sdk ac_apps/lib.lua](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/extension/internal/lua-sdk/ac_apps/lib.lua)). The changelog adds Real Mirrors that follow door rotation (0.2.1), door sound fixes (0.2.5) and deforming-hood fixes (0.2.8) ([CM CSP manifest](file:///C:/Users/nickl/AppData/Local/AcTools%20Content%20Manager/Temporary/Patch/Manifest.json)). `[DEFORMING_HOOD] NAME = MOTORHOOD` bends the bonnet in crashes ([CSP docs: Deforming bonnets](https://cup.acstuff.club/docs/csp/cars/deforming-bonnets)). Some features are missing altogether. **No getting-in or getting-out animation exists**: the Kunos driver clips are only steer and shift. No documented key ties a door sound to an `[ANIMATION_...]`. And `[WOBBLY_BIT_N]` has no hinge-angle limits, so it cannot fake a door that swings while driving ([CSP docs: Wobbly bits](https://cup.acstuff.club/docs/csp/cars/wobbly-bits)). Separately, the Nova's `driver3d.ini` names `steer.ksanim`, but the `animations/` folder holds only `.gitkeep` ([Nova car folder](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/car/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989)). That clip needs authoring alongside the panel clips.

## Cutting the panels means hinge empties, closed holes and inner skins

Kunos' naming is the safest base. `DOOR_L` and `DOOR_R` are "for exterior door animation (A and B)" and `DOOR_L_1`/`DOOR_R_1` "for interior door animation (A only)". `MOTORHOOD` and `REAR_HOOD` are damage nulls placed "in the PIVOT point of the object around which the object rotates upon impact" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). For bonnet and tailgate clips under CSP, the node name is free: real mods use `HOOD`, `TRUNK`, `FRONT_HOOD`, `boot`. Uniqueness is not free: "The AC engine does not support 2 OBJECTS with the same NAME in the same model. This will cause the game to crash" (same source), and clips find their nodes by name ([KsAnimAnimator.cs](https://github.com/gro-ove/actools/blob/master/AcTools.Render/Kn5Specific/Animations/KsAnimAnimator.cs)). The Kunos Miata shows the finished hierarchy. A `DOOR_L` dummy sits at the left front hinge with identity rotation. Under it are the door skin, door glass, handle, mirror, trim and the inward-facing interior glass. Under `COCKPIT_HR`, a second `DOOR_L001` dummy at the *same* hinge position carries the door card and rubber. The `MOTORHOOD` dummy sits at the bonnet's rear edge (0, 0.801, 0.526) with the skin, underside and plastics as children. One door clip moves the exterior and interior nulls together ([mazda_miata.kn5](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/ks_mazda_miata/mazda_miata.kn5)). The Nova kn5 today has every dummy at the origin and world-positioned meshes under `REF_BODY`, with no hinged node at all ([Nova kn5](file:///D:/Claude/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/car/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989/vauxhall_nova_gte_1989.kn5)). The target tree is:

```
ROOT
├─ REF_BODY ...            shell with door, bonnet and hatch apertures, jambs, closed bays
├─ DOOR_L   (empty on left front hinge axis, identity rotation)
│   └─ skin, inner skin, glass (outer + inward copy), frame, handle, mirror, seals
├─ DOOR_R   (mirror of DOOR_L; driver door on this RHD car)
├─ MOTORHOOD (empty on bonnet hinge line)
│   └─ BONNET_ANIM (optional inner empty so damage and animation don't fight)
│       └─ skin, underside, badge
├─ REAR_HOOD (empty on tailgate hinge line, roof rear edge)
│   └─ skin, inner frame, rear glass, wiper, badge, third light
└─ COCKPIT_HR
    ├─ DOOR_L_1 (same hinge as DOOR_L) └─ door card, pull, winder   (optional Kunos split)
    └─ DOOR_R_1 (same hinge as DOOR_R) └─ ...
```

The modelling sequence follows from Kunos' damage rules and from modders' practice. Start by marking each real shut line on the one-piece shell and making it a clean edge loop. Vehicle-modelling courses start the separation the same way, by cutting along an edge loop around each door ([LinkedIn Learning, excerpt](https://www.lynda.com/Blender-tutorials/Separating-hood-doors-trunk/180810/363907-4.html)). Then duplicate and separate the faces inside each loop, so the body keeps a matching hole edge. A raw cut is not enough. One Blender Artists user found that a door simply cut from a subdivided body "looked poor", and the advice was rolled, turned-in edges ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/how-to-make-car-door-apparent/337788)). Habrador's method gets there with modifiers: mark the gap edges sharp, put them in a vertex group, and stack Edge Split → Solidify → Bevel, limiting Solidify to the group because other sharp edges make it misbehave ([Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/10/adventures-making-doors-in-blender.html)). Next, build what an open panel reveals. "Make sure that you have closed the mesh in the interior. You put a black texture or something very dark to ensure that there is no gaping hole behind the moving objects", and "Detach parts only that don't leave holes in the car when moving, or carefully cap the holes" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). For the doors that means A- and B-pillar shut faces and the sill top. BeamNG practice treats the door as "the inverse of the door jamb" and keeps bonnet and boot undersides flat and simple ([BeamNG workflow](https://wiki.beamng.com/Vehicle_Creation_Workflow)). The bonnet needs the engine bay closed off beneath it, and the tailgate needs the boot floor, trims and seal flange. Every panel needs an inner skin facing inward. The Escort fix had to add body-colour inner door skins because "the outer skin faces outwards, so from the cabin AC would cull" it ([model_fixes.py](file:///D:/Claude/escort_mk2_dtm/tools/blender/model_fixes.py)), and Polycount critics flag doors whose top inner edge lets players see "into your model" ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/1208083)). Finally, add the empties at the hinges and parent everything that should move. The Escort precedent put an ARROWS empty "inside the doors' front edge" ([model_fixes.py](file:///D:/Claude/escort_mk2_dtm/tools/blender/model_fixes.py)). That includes the door glass (Kunos: interior glass "must be linked to the appropriate EXTERIOR door nulls"), the mirrors, emissive parts, and any CSP light, via `RELATIVE_TO`.

Several constraints shape the rest. **LODs:** Kunos welds doors into the body in LOD B, but a `MOTORHOOD` "if needed, must be present in LOD A and LOD B" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). An open door will therefore snap shut at LOD-switch distance, which is fine for pit use. The Escort precedent changed the main model only. **Collision is untouched,** because AC's collider is a separate closed `collider.kn5` of at most 40-60 triangles ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). **Damage versus animation** is unresolved. On Nik's Sunbeam base mod, a single `MOTORHOOD` node already serves vanilla damage wobble, CSP `[DEFORMING_HOOD]` and an opening clip ([Sunbeam backup damage.ini](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/content/cars/talbot_sunbeam_lotus_81_works_backup_v1/data/damage.ini)). But a clip overwrites the node's local matrix, and no source says how that interacts with damage rotation. The cautious design animates a child `BONNET_ANIM` empty instead, as one Blender modder's working two-level `HEADLIGHTS > HEADLIGHTS_ANIM` fix did ([assettocorsamods: FBX Animation](https://assettocorsamods.net/threads/fbx-animation.2498/)). For **AO**, Kunos bakes the cockpit with the doors open and "far enough to avoid a dark occlusion on the borders and the doorsill" ([Kunos Pipeline R2.0](file:///D:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/assettocorsa/sdk/dev/car_pipeline_2.0rev/AC_Pipeline_PUB_Rev2.0.pdf)). The test loop is short. Export the kn5 and the clips, open the car in CM's Custom Showroom, toggle the door and extra entries, and edit-and-re-export while CM hot-reloads. Only then check the CSP bindings in a session. Some inputs are still missing: the Nova's actual hinge positions, its bonnet hinge side and its opening angles were not researched and must come from the reference photos or a workshop manual. When judging the result with the doors open, apply the Polycount critique of one work-in-progress door: it was too thick and showed too large a hinge gap from inside, while a real door's section follows the window angle ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/1588865)).

## Conclusion

The two halves of this work collide on one fact: Blender stores custom normals relative to each smoothing fan. Cutting panels, adding turned-in edges and marking shut lines sharp all change those fans, so **normal transfer has to be the last geometry step, after the panels are cut, not before**. That suggests an order no single source states: make the smoothed twin from the *uncut* shell, then cut and separate the panels, then transfer from the uncut twin onto each panel with Nearest Face Interpolated mapping, a millimetre-scale Max Distance and the lip and return faces left out of the group. Reflections then flow continuously across the shut lines, which is what Polycount means by keeping the uncut mesh as the reference ([Polycount, excerpt](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2739861)). This costs Topology mapping's exactness, but on a smooth source every mapping measured the same residual. The cut is also the moment to face the budget: at about ten times Kunos' 125k exterior target, the shell already spans many 65k-vertex chunks, and jambs and inner skins add more.

The project is closer than the size of the question suggests. Its exporter already writes Blender's corner normals faithfully and triangulates without disturbing them, and Nik's Escort tooling already writes ksanim v2 with the right axis convention. What remains is modelling and two cheap guards: an assert that each clip's frame 0 matches the kn5 rest matrix, and a check that no body part is flagged `kn5_pristine`. The genuinely unknown items are few and testable in an evening: whether vanilla AC ever plays the door clips on its own, how a damage-wobbling `MOTORHOOD` behaves under an animation clip, and which way the Nova's bonnet hinges.
