# Texture-space AO pipeline and AO as livery shading

Scope: low-res AO bake and upsample, gutter fill (dilation / pull-push), in-island denoising, AO as a paint multiplier, pitfalls. Web research was thin on sim-racing livery AO specifics (several forum pages returned 403); those answers are mostly Inferences and flagged as such.

## (a) Low-res bake, then upsample to 4096 without seams

### Takeaway
No source documents an "upsample a low-res AO bake" workflow directly. Substance's docs describe dilation as a post-process after baking. The safe order that follows is: bake, fill the gutters, then resample, with island-aware weighting so no gutter or neighbouring-island texel leaks in.

### Cited Findings
- Substance 3D Bake describes dilation as a post-process after the bake. It extends island pixels outward by N px to avoid seams when borders don't align to the texel grid or when resolution is reduced (mipmaps). — [Substance Bake search summary](https://helpx.adobe.com/substance-3d-bake/bakers-settings/common-parameters.html) (page itself returned 403 to me; this is a search-result summary, medium confidence)
- Dark dots can appear on UV seam borders because the baker converts geometry to pixels. Dilation does not fix that kind of artefact. — [Substance Bake docs, via search summary](https://helpx.adobe.com/substance-3d-bake/common-issues/aliasing-on-uv-seams.html)
- Blender's old margin filter produced margin pixels much darker than the baked area, causing a visible seam. The new dilation function fixed this and the max margin went from 32 to 64 px. A faint seam was still reported by commenters. — [Blender code blog](https://code.blender.org/?p=510)
- Blender margin types: "Extend" extends border pixels outward. "Adjacent Faces" fills the margin from the neighbouring faces across UV seams, so the colour matches across the seam. Margin size is in pixels, and its purpose is to prevent seams from filtering and mip-mapping. — [Blender 4.0 manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.0/render/cycles/baking.html)
- Adjacent Faces can bleed pixels into the island interior (bug #119393). Extend is a pure image filter with no polygon info. Adjacent Faces causes dark halos on normal maps with rotated UVs, which Extend avoids. — [Blender issue #119393](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/119393); [issue #96942](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/96942)
- Unity's AO tooling filters baked AO by texel position, AO value and normal orientation (an edge-aware filter), as an alternative to plain blur. — [Unity Bake Ambient Occlusion docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.industry.toolkit@3.2/manual/Actions/Autogenerated/BakeAmbientOcclusion.html)

### Inferences
- AO is low-frequency, so a 1024 or 2048 bake upsampled to 4096 is visually fine on open panels. The risk is only at island borders and at sharp creases.
- Recommended order (my engineering inference, not a cited standard):
  1. Bake at N x N, then pull-push-fill the gutters at N.
  2. Upsample bilinearly or bicubically to 4096. Gutter texels now hold plausible AO, so island edges don't blend with black or empty texels.
  3. Re-mask: keep the result only inside the 4096 island coverage mask, then do a final pull-push fill at 4096 so the gutters match the high-res island edges.
  The alternative is normalised convolution: upsample (colour x coverage) and coverage separately and divide. This is equivalent in effect and needs no pre-fill.
- Because the 4096 coverage mask is sharper than the low-res one, texels at the island rim of the 4096 map may fall where the low-res mask was empty. Pre-filling the low-res gutters guarantees valid data there.

### Gaps
- No source found that documents upsampling AO specifically, or a recommended padding at the low-res stage.

## (b) Filling empty texels: dilation, padding and pull-push

### Takeaway
Standard practice is to fill the gutter by 1 px per mip level, so 16 px covers about the base plus 3 mips. Substance's "dilation infinite" fills the whole empty space. Pull-push gives a smooth fill, but there is no authoritative source saying it beats nearest-neighbour dilation for mipmaps. That claim is reasoning only.

### Cited Findings
- Gortler et al. 1996 (The Lumigraph) introduced pull-push. The pull phase builds a pyramid of data values and positive weights. The push phase goes coarse to fine and blends the results into each level. — [Kraus 2009 summary via search](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2009/17726/); [Gortler 96](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr01/cs598b/papers/gortler96.pdf)
- Kraus notes the original equations lack clamping, and revises pull and push for correctness and GPU speed. I could not extract the equations; the PDF was binary. — [Kraus, Pull-Push Revisited](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/cg/Research/Publications/2009/Pull-Push_Algorithm/grapp09.pdf)
- Lefebvre et al. used a GPU pull-push for inpainting gaps in texture maps (snippet only). — search summary of the Kraus paper above
- A simple pyramid variant: average only non-hole pixels of each 2x2 block on the way down, then replace holes with upscaled coarser data on the way up. — [golanlevin/image_inpainting_processing](https://github.com/golanlevin/image_inpainting_processing) (via search summary)
- Substance Painter padding modes: no padding; "dilation infinite" stretches island borders until they meet another island or the texture edge; "dilation with transparency" stretches by a set pixel count. Adobe says good padding matters for mipmap generation, and lower mips otherwise pull in background or other-area colours. The page gives no pixel numbers. — [Adobe Substance Painter: Texture dilation or padding](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d-painter/using/technical-support/workflow-issues/export-issues/texture-dilation-or-padding)
- Blender developer Andy's 2013 reasoning: you need at least 1 px of margin per UV island per mip level. A 16 px margin covers the base bake plus three mips (16, 8, 4, 2 px). He says a margin that removes the background colour entirely would be ideal. The old 2 px default only covered the initial bake. — [Blender bf-committers 2013](https://archive.blender.org/lists/bf-committers/2013-February/039281.html)
- Polycount rules of thumb: 16 texels covers about 4 mips if neighbouring colours are similar, and 32 px was suggested for 4K. Padding is how far the island is bled out, not the gap between islands. This is forum opinion from a search summary; I could not fetch the page (403). — [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/191707/padding)
- Unity exposes `maxMipLevelPadding` for atlases, showing engines treat per-mip padding as a first-class concept. — [Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.core@15.0/api/UnityEngine.Rendering.Texture2DAtlas.maxMipLevelPadding.html)
- The Tripo blog formula "2^(mip levels)/2" is implausible (1024 px for 2K) and should not be used. — [Tripo blog](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/smart-mesh-uv-padding-rules-to-prevent-mip-bleeding)

### Inferences
- Padding sizing: 4096 down to mip 4 (256) needs about 16 px, and down to mip 6 about 64 px. If the gutters will be fully filled anyway (the AO map is just a multiplier), the cheapest robust answer is to fill every empty texel, the equivalent of "dilation infinite". Pull-push does that in O(N) and cannot run out of padding.
- Why pull-push beats nearest-neighbour dilation (my reasoning, uncited): nearest-neighbour fill copies one edge texel along a stripe pattern. That gives hard discontinuities between stripes and, in the gutter between two islands, an arbitrary choice of whichever island is nearer. Pull-push gives a smooth blend of nearby valid data. For a low-frequency AO multiplier, smooth is better, since mip averaging of a smooth fill barely changes the island's edge colour.
- Pull-push numpy sketch (standard algorithm, written from the descriptions above):
  ```
  # img: HxWxC float, w: HxW coverage in [0,1]
  levels=[(img*w[...,None], w)]
  while min(shape)>1:
      c,w = levels[-1]
      c = c.reshape(H/2,2,W/2,2,C).sum((1,3)); w = w.reshape(H/2,2,W/2,2).sum((1,3))   # pull: sum of 2x2
      levels.append((c, np.minimum(w, k)))  # optional clamp (Kraus)
  # push: from coarsest, colour = c/w where w>0
  fill = c_top/w_top
  for each finer level: up = fill.repeat(2,0).repeat(2,1)   # or bilinear
      own = c/w where w>0; a = clip(w,0,1)
      fill = a*own + (1-a)*up
  ```
  Keep original texels where coverage is 1, so only holes change. Use bilinear (not repeat) upsampling in the push for smoothness.
- Do the fill in the final stored space (the AO multiplier), not on colour that will later be tinted.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source for "pull-push vs nearest dilation for mipmaps". Kraus's equations were not readable.
- No game-engine documentation giving pixels-per-mip numbers beyond the 2013 Blender post and forum opinion.

## (c) Denoising and smoothing AO in texture space

### Takeaway
Few-direction AO is noisy. The Substance AO baker lowers noise by raising its Quality setting (3, 5, 10 or 16 passes). For the CPU, the practical route is more samples, then an island-aware blur.

### Cited Findings
- Substance AO baker Quality: Low 3, Medium 5 (default), High 10, Very High 16 passes. Precision Bias lower means more precision but larger artefacts. Distance Fade sets the spread. Raising Quality is the stated way to reduce noise. — [Substance Bake AO settings](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d/bakers/bakers-settings/ambient-occlusion)
- Unity filters AO using texel position, AO value and normal orientation, an edge-preserving approach. — [Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.industry.toolkit@3.2/manual/Actions/Autogenerated/BakeAmbientOcclusion.html)
- An Overtake poster reports CSP-generated AO coming out blocky/textured, with smudge and blur not fixing it. — [Overtake via search summary](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/alternative-ways-to-generate-higher-resolution-ao-maps.157626/) (single anecdote, page not fetched)

### Inferences
- Blur only inside islands: use normalised convolution. Blur (AO x mask) and mask with the same Gaussian, then divide. Texels outside the island contribute nothing, so no cross-island bleed. Do it before the gutter fill.
- To avoid smoothing across creases, make the blur bilateral on world-space normal or position, weighting by similarity. Computing this in texel space needs a per-texel 3D position and normal map, which a texel-to-3D map provides.
- A radius of about 1 to 2 percent of the texture width, at the low-res bake resolution, is a reasonable starting point (my estimate, not sourced).
- Also blur in the sampling domain, not only the image domain: with few rays, jitter ray directions per texel (blue-noise or Cranley-Patterson rotation), so the noise is high-frequency and the blur removes it.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a recommended blur radius or ray count for car AO.

## (d) AO as the shading under a livery

### Takeaway
Multiply is the standard blend for AO. Beyond that, the sources found give no numbers. In AC, stock cars carry baked AO in a separate texture and shader channel, so a livery generator mostly decides whether to bake AO into the diffuse. The 50-80 % opacity range was not found in any source.

### Cited Findings
- Substance docs: AO is grayscale, and the recommended blend modes are Normal (paint over) or Multiply (combine). — [Substance Painter: Ambient Occlusion Painting](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d-painter/using/painting/advanced-channel-painting/ambient-occlusion-painting)
- A forum poster (LFS) found multiply at 100 % dull and reduced layer opacity to tone it down. Anecdote. — [LFS forum](https://www.lfs.net/forum/post/420439)
- AC: default Kunos AO content is usually just the AO bake with the green channel fully white (a forum claim). Content Manager's custom showroom can generate AO maps at different resolutions. A poster believed AO is applied as a multiply on the skin; non-expert guess. Another advises against VAO on cars that already have baked AO. — [Overtake threads via search summary](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/alternative-ways-to-generate-higher-resolution-ao-maps.157626/)
- iRacing templates ship with spec-map layer groups; layer blending options are inspectable in the template. No AO-layer guidance was found. — [iRacing Paint Textures](https://www.iracing.com/painttextures/)
- Trading Paints notes that "Car Parts" layers (lights, stamped logos) can be displayed above or below custom artwork, which affects where a shading layer sits. — [Trading Paints help](https://help.tradingpaints.com/paint-builder/using-paint-builders-painting-guides/)

### Inferences
- Formula: `out = livery * (1 - s * (1 - ao_norm))`, with ao_norm = ao / p95(ao) clipped to 1, and strength s in roughly 0.4 to 0.8 (my choice, in line with the anecdote that 100 % looks dull). Normalising to the 95th percentile (or median of open panels) makes open panels about 1.0, so only creases, arches and gaps darken.
- Apply a contrast curve (ao^gamma, gamma about 1.5 to 2) so mild occlusion stays near 1 and only deep occlusion darkens.
- Colour space: AO from a ray-caster is linear. If the livery is sRGB-encoded 8-bit and the multiply is done directly on the encoded values, it darkens more strongly than a linear multiply would, roughly like a gamma 2.2 power on the occlusion. For physically plausible results convert to linear, multiply, convert back; for an artistic multiplier, do it in sRGB and tune s by eye. Be consistent. I could not find a source stating which AC or CM paint shops use.
- Do not multiply by white-tinted AO where the livery is metallic or flake: apply AO to the diffuse only, and let the game's own AO (the stock AO texture) handle the rest, or the car will get double occlusion if the shader already multiplies AO.
- Since in-game shaders already apply the car's AO map (likely), baking AO into the diffuse risks double darkening; keep s modest. (Inference from the forum claim above, unverified.)

### Gaps
- No source found for a typical iRacing, rFactor 2 or Content Manager paint-shop AO opacity.
- No source on whether the AC car shader already multiplies the AO map into the final colour. Verify against the kn5 shader/ksEditor if it matters.

## (e) Pitfalls

### Takeaway
Most pitfalls follow from rays hitting geometry that should not count, or from UVs sharing texels. The sources cover the margin and seam side; the geometric side is my reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- Blender Adjacent Faces margin can bleed into the island interior, and Extend avoids that. — [Blender issue #119393](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/119393)
- Blender margin can overlap when multiple meshes are baked together. — [Blender issue #83971](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/83971)
- Substance AO baker has a "Use Unselected Mesh Parts" option so other parts can occlude; and an "Invert Normal" mode that computes AO from inside, which gives a thickness map. — [Substance AO settings](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d/bakers/bakers-settings/ambient-occlusion)

### Inferences
- Inside-of-panel AO: cast rays only on the outer side (from the front-face normal side). If a panel is single-sided with its normal pointing out, a ray from the outer face can pass through the unfilled inside. Close the mesh, or ignore back-facing hits, so the interior does not darken the whole outside.
- Interior seen through windows: window glass must not occlude (treat as transparent for rays) and the interior should not be the occluder for exterior texels; bake exterior and interior as separate passes or per-material, with glass excluded.
- Overlapping or mirrored UVs: two surfaces sharing a texel have different true AO. Options: average them; use the lower value (conservative, darker); or the one on the visible/outer side. For left/right mirrored parts, averaging is usually fine because the car is nearly symmetric. For stacked islands (for example inner and outer door skins), do not average; prefer the outer surface.
- Dark seams on island edges: partial-coverage rim texels average island data with empty or zero texels. Use normalised convolution (divide by coverage) and a conservative coverage mask (erode by 1 texel) before the fill.
- Do not let the multiplier pull the livery's own near-black areas, or flat-black wraps, further down; clamp the output floor.

### Gaps
- No documented industry convention for mirrored-UV AO choice.
